## 6. Draft LinkedIn caption for Jeff review

The biggest AI implementation mistake may be delegating the wrong decision.

TechCrunch reported this morning that Anthropic-backed Ode is building forward-deployed engineering teams to help companies redesign priority products and business processes around AI.

That is a useful market signal for PE operating partners and portfolio-company leaders:

The model may not be the bottleneck anymore. Implementation capacity is.

But before an outside team touches a core workflow, settle five decision rights:

1. **Workflow ownership**  
Who can change the process—and who owns the business result after it changes?

2. **Access boundaries**  
Which systems, data and actions are in scope? Who can grant, narrow or revoke access?

3. **Model authority**  
Who decides when to use a different model or vendor, and what test controls that choice?

4. **Acceptance authority**  
Who defines “good enough,” runs the evaluation and can stop the rollout?

5. **Internal handoff**  
What must the company's team be able to operate, maintain and improve without the implementation partner?

If every answer is “the implementation partner,” the company has outsourced more than engineering.

If every answer is “a committee,” the project may never ship.

The practical middle is a named business owner, bounded technical authority, a measurable acceptance test and an internal team that can run what gets built.

That is how outside AI talent becomes operating leverage instead of permanent dependence.

Educational only; not legal, investment or technical advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
