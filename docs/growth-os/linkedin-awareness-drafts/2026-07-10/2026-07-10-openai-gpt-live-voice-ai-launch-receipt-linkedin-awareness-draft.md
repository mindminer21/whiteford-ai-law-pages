## 6. Draft LinkedIn caption for Jeff review

Voice AI is not just a new interface.

It changes the operating surface of the product.

OpenAI’s new GPT-Live models can listen and speak at the same time. The system can decide whether to talk, keep listening, pause, interrupt, or invoke a tool while the conversation continues.

That is a meaningful product shift.

Product-leader translation:

Before a voice feature reaches customers, build a **voice AI launch receipt**.

One page. Five rows.

1. **Product promise** — What job is voice supposed to improve? What may the system say, recommend, translate, record, retrieve, or do? What remains explicitly out of scope?

2. **Conversation boundary** — How will users know they are interacting with AI? When is listening active? What happens during silence, interruption, background speech, a failed handoff, or a sensitive conversation?

3. **Data path** — What audio, transcript, metadata, memory, tool result, and customer record is created? Where does each item go, who can access it, and when is it deleted?

4. **Action boundary** — Which tools may the system call? Which actions require confirmation? What triggers a human handoff, a stop, or a rollback?

5. **Release evidence** — Which model/version, evaluation set, languages, failure cases, customer promises, incident owner, monitoring signals, and rollback plan support the launch?

The model demo proves the conversation can happen.

The launch receipt proves the company knows what happens next.

That is the difference between “we added voice” and “we can operate voice.”

For teams building that operating map, I keep a free AI Compliance Gameplan at https://learn.schellip.com/ai-compliance-gameplan.

Educational only; not legal advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
