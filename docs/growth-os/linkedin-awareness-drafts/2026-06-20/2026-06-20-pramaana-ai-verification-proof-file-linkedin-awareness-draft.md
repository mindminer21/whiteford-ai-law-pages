## 5. Draft LinkedIn post in Jeff’s concise educational style

Draft only — attorney review required before any manual use. No legal advice. No attorney-client relationship. Not a comment on Pramaana’s legal position, product readiness, investors, customers, or any company’s legal/compliance posture.

> A lot of AI products are stuck in the same uncomfortable place:
>
> impressive demo, unclear proof.
>
> Pramaana Labs’ June 17 funding announcement is a useful signal. The company raised a $27M seed round led by Khosla Ventures to build an AI verification layer for high-stakes work — the kind of environment where “the answer sounds right” is not enough.
>
> Whether or not formal verification is part of your stack, the operator lesson is broader:
>
> If an AI feature is moving from pilot to production, someone needs to own the proof file.
>
> I’d call it an **AI Verification Proof File**.
>
> It answers seven plain-English questions:
>
> 1. **Use case** — what decision, workflow, customer promise, or internal task is the AI actually supporting?
>
> 2. **Allowed scope** — what is the system allowed to answer, recommend, generate, approve, change, or escalate?
>
> 3. **Source of truth** — which policies, contracts, rules, clinical guidelines, technical specs, customer terms, datasets, or human approvals control the answer?
>
> 4. **Assumptions** — what facts did the system assume, and where did those facts come from?
>
> 5. **Failure boundary** — when should the AI refuse, hand off, ask for more information, or flag uncertainty instead of producing a polished answer?
>
> 6. **Human review** — who reviews what, at what threshold, with what authority to override, pause, or ship?
>
> 7. **Evidence trail** — can the team reconstruct the inputs, rules, model/tool calls, human approvals, output, and customer-facing representation later?
>
> This is not just an AI governance exercise.
>
> It is product discipline.
>
> It helps sales answer enterprise procurement questions.
>
> It helps engineering avoid brittle launches.
>
> It helps legal and compliance understand what is actually being promised.
>
> It helps investors and board members separate a real product motion from a demo with risk hidden in the margins.
>
> A practical test for this week:
>
> Pick one AI feature that is closest to customer, revenue, regulated, or operationally important use.
>
> Then ask: “Could a new product leader understand what this system is allowed to do, what it relies on, when it stops, and how we prove what happened — without interviewing five people and reading six Slack threads?”
>
> If not, the gap probably is not another policy.
>
> It is the missing proof file.
>
> Educational only; not legal advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
>
> [Optional company tag candidate if Jeff manually verifies: Pramaana Labs]
