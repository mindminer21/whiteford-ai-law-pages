> Employee-facing AI needs a trust map before it scales.
>
> Wellbees is a useful current example. Its June 22 announcement says the AI-powered employee wellbeing platform raised a $3.6M Series A led by Qatar Development Bank to support Gulf expansion, a new Qatar office, regional team growth, and more investment in software, product development, and AI capabilities.
>
> The operator lesson is broader than one company:
>
> When AI touches employee wellbeing, HR insights, expert support, engagement, rewards, or people analytics, the real question is not just:
>
> “Does the product personalize the experience?”
>
> It is:
>
> “Can the company explain the path from employee input to AI suggestion to human support to employer insight?”
>
> I’d build a **people-data trust floorplan** before the next enterprise customer, board member, investor, or buyer asks for it.
>
> Five rooms on the floorplan:
>
> 1. **Employee inputs** — what data enters the product, and what should never enter?
>
> 2. **AI personalization** — what does the AI recommend, rank, summarize, nudge, or infer?
>
> 3. **Expert-support boundary** — when does a person, coach, clinician, EAP resource, or other support path step in, and what stays outside the software’s role?
>
> 4. **HR insight window** — what can the employer see, what is aggregated, what is individual-level, and who has access?
>
> 5. **Diligence file** — can the team show the notices, data flows, vendor terms, security posture, model/tool roles, human review points, and customer-facing promises in one place?
>
> This is useful even if you are not in HR tech.
>
> Any founder building AI into employee workflows — onboarding, productivity, training, benefits, safety, performance support, workforce planning — is eventually selling trust architecture, not just software.
>
> A practical test for this week:
>
> Pick the AI feature closest to employee data.
>
> Then ask: “Could a skeptical enterprise buyer understand what data moves, what AI does, what humans review, what the company promises, and what evidence we can produce — without five follow-up calls?”
>
> If not, the gap may not be the model.
>
> It may be the missing floorplan.
>
> Educational only; not legal advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
>
