## 5. Draft LinkedIn post in Jeff’s concise educational style

Draft only — attorney review required before any manual use. No legal advice. No attorney-client relationship. Not a comment on Copia’s legal position or any company’s compliance/security posture.

> The most important plant code may be the least diligence-ready.
>
> Copia’s June 16 funding announcement is a useful market signal: industrial teams are paying more attention to OT code management, PLC backups, and recovery workflows.
>
> The broader lesson for CTOs, engineering leaders, CISOs, and industrial operators is not “buy a tool.”
>
> It is this:
>
> If automation code runs the line, the recovery record is part of the business record.
>
> A simple OT recovery vault should answer five questions:
>
> 1. **Known-good backup** — what PLC / HMI / robot / device logic can we actually restore?
>
> 2. **Change history** — who changed what, when, why, from which vendor tool, and with what approval?
>
> 3. **Access map** — which employees, contractors, integrators, vendors, remote paths, and admin accounts can touch the system?
>
> 4. **Contract alignment** — do our uptime promises, warranties, indemnities, cyber representations, insurance assumptions, and incident-notice duties match reality?
>
> 5. **Ownership and proof** — can we show assignments, contractor rights, trade-secret controls, test restores, and the reasoning behind emergency changes?
>
> This is not just a cyber issue.
>
> It becomes a customer-trust issue, an insurance-renewal issue, a financing issue, an M&A diligence issue, and sometimes an IP / trade-secret issue.
>
> A good test for this week:
>
> Could your team restore the line — and prove what changed — without having to reconstruct the story from memory?
>
> Educational only; not legal advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
>
