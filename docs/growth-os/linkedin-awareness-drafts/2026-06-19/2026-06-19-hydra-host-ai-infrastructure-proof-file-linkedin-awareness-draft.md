> AI infrastructure companies are learning a simple lesson: capital is not the same thing as readiness.
>
> Hydra Host’s June 15 announcement is a useful market signal — a Boulder AI infrastructure company raising a $100M Series A to expand GPU-as-a-Service and global AI factory capacity.
>
> The interesting founder/GC/CTO takeaway is not “go raise a big round.”
>
> It is this:
>
> When the business spans software, GPUs, data centers, customer workloads, procurement, performance claims, security promises, and global operations, the diligence file has to keep up with the product.
>
> I’d want an **AI Infrastructure Proof File** before the next enterprise customer review, board update, financing round, insurance renewal, or M&A conversation.
>
> A useful proof file answers seven questions:
>
> 1. **System map** — what is software, what is hardware, what is third-party infrastructure, and what is the company’s own technical edge?
>
> 2. **Customer promise map** — what are sales, SLAs, support, uptime, security, data, and performance materials actually promising?
>
> 3. **Data/workload boundary** — whose workloads, prompts, outputs, logs, telemetry, and operational data touch the platform, and under what terms?
>
> 4. **Vendor and operator stack** — which data-center, GPU, cloud, network, reseller, and supply-chain contracts make the system work?
>
> 5. **Ownership record** — who created the software, orchestration, deployment workflows, technical documentation, branding, and know-how?
>
> 6. **IP / trade secret / contract split** — what belongs in patents, what stays confidential, what is protected by contracts, and what has already been publicly disclosed?
>
> 7. **Diligence narrative** — can the CEO, CTO, GC, sales, finance, and board tell the same clean story without reconstructing it from Slack, Notion, GitHub, and old decks?
>
> This is not just a legal exercise.
>
> It is how a technical company turns momentum into buyer/investor/customer trust.
>
> A practical test for this week:
>
> Pick one major product or infrastructure claim on the website or in the sales deck. Then ask: “Could we prove the technical, contractual, data, ownership, and customer-support facts behind this claim in one clean folder?”
>
> If not, the next step probably is not a memo.
>
> It is an operating-record cleanup.
>
> For teams that want a non-confidential starting point, I keep a free Colorado Tech Company Legal Health Checklist here: https://learn.schellip.com/colorado-tech-general-counsel
>
> Educational only; not legal advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
