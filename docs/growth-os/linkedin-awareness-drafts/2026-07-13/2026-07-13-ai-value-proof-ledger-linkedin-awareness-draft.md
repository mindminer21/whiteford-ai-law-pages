## 6. Draft LinkedIn caption for Jeff review

The AI pitch deck is entering its “show me” phase.

A Crunchbase interview published today with Sapphire Ventures partner Anders Ranum captured the shift well:

Show me how AI helps you win.

Show me whether the product is embedded in the customer’s actual work.

Show me monetization, trust, auditability, and a cost the buyer can predict.

That is consistent with Deloitte’s 2026 enterprise AI survey: 66% of organizations reported productivity or efficiency gains, but only 20% reported increased revenue.

Founder translation:

The demo is no longer the proof.

Build an **AI value proof ledger** with five rows:

1. **Workflow change** — What task, decision, handoff, or customer outcome works differently because of the product? Is AI doing part of the work, or merely sitting beside the old process?

2. **Measured result** — What baseline and post-deployment evidence show time saved, revenue created, loss avoided, quality improved, downtime reduced, or capacity added?

3. **Repeatability** — Did the result survive a second team, customer, dataset, location, workflow, or model version? What breaks outside the happy-path demo?

4. **Cost to serve** — What do inference, vendor, human review, support, exception handling, and infrastructure cost per successful outcome? Can the customer predict the bill as usage scales?

5. **Enterprise dependence** — Which integrations, permissions, controls, records, review steps, and operating routines make the product trusted and difficult to remove?

The useful test:

Could the same five-row file support the next renewal, board update, pricing decision, and financing conversation without changing the underlying story?

If not, the company may have AI activity—but not yet an AI value record.

Educational only; not legal advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
