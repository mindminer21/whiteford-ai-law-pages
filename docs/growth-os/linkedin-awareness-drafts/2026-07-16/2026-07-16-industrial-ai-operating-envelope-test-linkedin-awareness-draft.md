## 6. Draft LinkedIn caption for Jeff review

A model that can see the whole plant is still deployed one decision at a time.

TechCrunch reported late yesterday that Applied Computing raised $20 million for Orbital, an industrial AI system designed to combine time-series data, physics and engineering context to forecast facility conditions and simulate how one change may affect the rest of the operation.

For product and operations leaders, the practical question is not:

“How much data can the model ingest?”

It is:

“Which decision changes on Monday morning—and how will we know that change deserves to stay?”

Before moving an industrial AI pilot from observation to recommendation, run this five-question **operating-envelope test**:

1. **Decision**  
What exact operator decision will change? Who owns the business result—not just the model output?

2. **Conditions**  
Under which equipment states, product mixes, environmental conditions and data-quality levels is the recommendation in scope? When should the system stay silent or escalate?

3. **Baseline**  
What current method is the model being compared against? Which measure matters: yield, energy use, downtime, quality, response time or something else?

4. **Adjacent effects**  
If the team follows the recommendation, what happens elsewhere in the process? Which constraints must remain true before the change is accepted?

5. **Authority**  
Who validates, overrides, pauses and rolls back the recommendation? What new evidence is required before expanding to another site, shift, process step or degree of autonomy?

If the team can demo the prediction but cannot answer these five questions, the next milestone is not broader deployment.

It is a narrower, better-instrumented decision.

The goal is not to make operators trust AI.

It is to make each recommendation earn the next degree of authority.

Educational only; not legal, safety, engineering or investment advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
