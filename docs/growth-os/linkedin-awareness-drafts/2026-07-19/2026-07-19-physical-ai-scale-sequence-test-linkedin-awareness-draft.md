## 6. Draft LinkedIn caption for Jeff review

A bigger facility is not a scale plan.

Neither is a financing, a hiring announcement or a full pipeline.

Scale happens when those moves form a sequence.

Agility Robotics offered a useful public example this week. The company opened a 60,000-square-foot Fremont hub for software and physical-AI development while preparing for a proposed public-company transaction.

Agility says the new site will complement manufacturing in Oregon, support nearly 200 planned hires, and help expand the capabilities used in customer deployments. It also reports more than $300 million in multi-year orders, subject to contractual milestones, and a pipeline of more than 30 customers.

Those are company-reported figures—not a conclusion about the company or an investment view.

But the operating pattern is worth studying.

Before a deep-tech leadership team approves its next major expansion, test five links:

1. **Customer constraint**  
What is preventing wider adoption today: performance, reliability, integration, safety, economics, deployment time or something else?

2. **Capability**  
Which specific product or operating capability removes that constraint?

3. **Deployment result**  
What result in a real customer environment would show that the capability works outside the lab?

4. **Production constraint**  
If demand follows, what becomes the bottleneck: components, people, quality, installation, service, working capital or customer change management?

5. **Next milestone**  
What measurable event should occur before the company adds the next layer of fixed cost or makes the next public commitment?

Then inspect the arrows.

Can the team explain how the capital funds the capability, how the capability changes deployment results, and how those results justify production expansion?

If not, the company may be scaling cost faster than learning.

The best scale story is not the one with the most impressive inputs.

It is the one where every major commitment removes the constraint controlling the next milestone.

Educational only; not legal, securities, investment, financing, robotics, safety, manufacturing, employment, tax or engineering advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
