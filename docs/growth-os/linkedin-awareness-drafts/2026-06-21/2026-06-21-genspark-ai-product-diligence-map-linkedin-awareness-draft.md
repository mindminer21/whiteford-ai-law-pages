## 5. Draft LinkedIn post in Jeff’s concise educational style

Draft only — attorney review required before any manual use. No legal advice. No attorney-client relationship. Not a comment on Genspark’s legal position, product readiness, investors, customers, or any company’s legal/compliance posture.

> Investors do not need another AI demo.
>
> They need a way to tell whether the demo can survive customers, board questions, enterprise procurement, and the next financing process.
>
> Genspark’s June 17 funding announcement is a useful signal. Reuters reported that the AI workspace company raised a $100M extension at a $2.6B valuation, in a market still rewarding AI products aimed at workplace productivity and automation.
>
> The operator lesson is bigger than one company:
>
> As AI products move from “cool output” to “real workflow,” the diligence question changes.
>
> It is no longer just:
>
> “Can the system generate slides, models, apps, dashboards, research, or customer-facing work?”
>
> It becomes:
>
> “Can the company explain how that work is produced, bounded, reviewed, supported, and trusted?”
>
> I’d ask for an **AI Product Diligence Map**.
>
> Seven plain-English sections:
>
> 1. **Workflow served** — what business workflow, customer job, or internal process is the AI actually improving?
>
> 2. **Output promise** — what deliverables can the product reliably create, and what should it not be expected to create?
>
> 3. **Model/tool chain** — which models, APIs, connectors, datasets, human steps, and third-party services sit behind the output?
>
> 4. **Source of truth** — what data, customer inputs, documents, systems, or policies does the product rely on?
>
> 5. **Review points** — where does a human approve, correct, escalate, or stop the workflow before it reaches a customer or business-critical use?
>
> 6. **Failure boundary** — when does the product say “I cannot do that,” ask for more context, downgrade confidence, or hand off?
>
> 7. **Evidence trail** — can the team reconstruct the prompt/input, tool path, assumptions, human approvals, final output, and customer-facing representation later?
>
> This is useful for investors.
>
> It is also useful for founders.
>
> If you are raising, selling into enterprise, preparing a board update, or cleaning up a data room, this map turns a scattered AI product story into something a buyer, investor, GC, CTO, or customer can actually evaluate.
>
> A practical test for this week:
>
> Pick the AI workflow closest to revenue.
>
> Then ask: “Could a new board member or enterprise customer understand what this product does, what it depends on, where humans stay in the loop, and how we prove what happened — without five follow-up calls?”
>
> If not, the gap may not be the model.
>
> It may be the missing diligence map.
>
> Educational only; not legal advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
>
> [Optional company tag candidate if Jeff manually verifies: Genspark]
