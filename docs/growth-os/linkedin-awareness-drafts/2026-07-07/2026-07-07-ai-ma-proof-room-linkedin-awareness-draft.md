## 5. Draft LinkedIn caption for Jeff review

The AI deal memo cannot stop at the demo.

Bain’s midyear M&A report says global deal value was up 41% year-over-year through the first five months of 2026, with megadeals leading the market.

PwC’s midyear outlook makes the other half of the point: AI is changing not only what buyers acquire, but how target screening, diligence, valuation, investment-committee prep, and value-creation planning get done.

Investor translation:

If AI is part of the deal thesis, the company needs a proof room before the banker asks.

Not a pitch deck.
Not a folder of random contracts.
Not a demo script.

A short operating record that answers:

1. What AI capability is the buyer or investor actually underwriting?
2. Where does AI touch product, engineering, operations, sales, support, finance, security, or customer delivery?
3. Which datasets, models, vendors, prompts, workflows, human reviews, and customer promises support the story?
4. Who owns each diligence answer and each first-100-day integration workstream?
5. What must be preserved, measured, integrated, or left alone after close so the value thesis survives operations?

This is where the IP file becomes one part of the broader business file.

Ownership, product claims, data rights, contracts, technical records, customer proof, and the integration plan all need to tell the same story.

Which part of your AI deal thesis would be hardest to prove in week one of diligence?

Educational only; not legal advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
