## 6. Draft LinkedIn caption for Jeff review

A grant application is a product strategy compression test.

If the team cannot explain how one experiment changes one commercial decision, the proposal may be describing activity rather than progress.

That question is timely in Colorado.

CU's Lab Venture Challenge has moved past its July 17 innovation-disclosure deadline. Eligible teams now face a July 24 pre-award-administrator deadline and an August 21 application deadline while competing for grants of up to $125,000.

CU says the program looks for a commercial need, a clear path to a compelling market and strong scientific support.

For a product leader working with a university spinout, I would translate those criteria into five questions:

1. **Who changes behavior?**  
Name the specific user, buyer, partner or adopter. What do they do today, and why is the current alternative not good enough?

2. **Which decision is blocked?**  
What cannot be decided yet: product design, target market, pilot scope, licensing path, manufacturing route, partnership or next financing step?

3. **What result would unblock it?**  
Choose the smallest technical or customer experiment that could materially change that decision—not merely produce more interesting data.

4. **Why is this the right spend now?**  
Connect each major budget item to an uncertainty it reduces. If an expense does not change a decision, challenge it.

5. **What happens next?**  
State the action the team will take if the result is positive, negative or ambiguous. A milestone should open or close a path.

Then read the sequence aloud:

**customer need → blocked decision → experiment → spend → next action**

If one arrow is weak, fix that before polishing the narrative.

And for university teams, public disclosure needs its own timing conversation with the institution's licensing team; market learning and protection planning should move together, not in separate lanes.

The strongest commercialization proposal is not the one with the most ambitious experiment.

It is the one where the result changes what the team does next.

Educational only; not legal, grant, investment, accounting, tax, regulatory, scientific, engineering or commercialization advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
