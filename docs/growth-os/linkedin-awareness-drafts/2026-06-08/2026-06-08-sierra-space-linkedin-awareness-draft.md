A Colorado space company like [Company tag candidate: Sierra Space — Jeff to verify manually before tagging] is a useful public example of a broader deep-tech issue:

When a technical system combines spacecraft hardware, propulsion, software, telemetry/command workflows, manufacturing/test infrastructure, and mission evidence, the “invention record” cannot live in one notebook or one patent disclosure form.

The useful map is usually layered:

1. What changed in the system architecture?
2. Who contributed the technical idea, reduction-to-practice work, test design, and implementation details?
3. Which evidence sits in CAD, code, test logs, supplier docs, mission-control workflows, demos, and customer/public disclosures?
4. What belongs in a patent discussion, what belongs in trade-secret discipline, and what belongs in contracts or publication controls?

This is not a comment on Sierra Space’s legal position, and it is not legal advice. It is just a practical issue deep-tech leaders may want to think about before the legal conversation starts.
