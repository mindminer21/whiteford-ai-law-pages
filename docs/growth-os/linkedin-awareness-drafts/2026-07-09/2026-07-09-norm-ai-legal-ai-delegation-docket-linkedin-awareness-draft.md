## 6. Draft LinkedIn caption for Jeff review

Legal AI is becoming an operating model, not a chatbot.

Norm Ai announced this week that it raised a $120M Series C at a $1.2B valuation, led by Khosla Ventures.

The interesting part is not only the funding.

It is the operating model: AI agents, senior attorney supervision, legal/compliance workflows, and even supervisory agents for regulated enterprise AI deployments.

GC / CTO translation:

If your company is buying, building, or piloting legal or regulatory AI, the question is not just “does the demo work?”

The better question is:

Can we show the delegation record?

Before legal AI touches higher-stakes workflows, I would want a simple **legal AI delegation docket** that answers:

1. **Authority boundary** — what may the AI read, summarize, draft, recommend, route, approve, send, file, negotiate, or never do without a human?

2. **Supervision map** — who owns the workflow, who reviews outputs, when is escalation mandatory, and what exceptions stop the process?

3. **Source + policy trail** — which playbooks, templates, laws, customer rules, data sources, vendor terms, and version histories does the system rely on?

4. **Outcome receipt** — what prompt, tool, model/version, input source, output, reviewer, decision, timestamp, and final business action were recorded?

5. **Procurement proof** — do the product claims, privacy/security commitments, customer terms, vendor terms, insurance assumptions, and incident-response plan match the workflow actually being deployed?

That docket is not bureaucracy.

It is the file that lets legal, product, security, finance, and the board talk about AI delegation with the same facts.

The companies that get this right will not just have better demos.

They will have AI workflows that are easier to approve, supervise, insure, diligence, sell, and scale.

Educational only; not legal advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
