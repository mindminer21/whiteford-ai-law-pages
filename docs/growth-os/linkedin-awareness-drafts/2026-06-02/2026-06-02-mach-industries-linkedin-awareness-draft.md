Mach Industries is a useful public example of a broader defense-tech pattern: autonomous systems companies are scaling product, manufacturing, government, and commercial lanes at the same time.

That creates a practical operator question — not a company-specific legal conclusion:

When AI-enabled or autonomous products move from prototype to production, who owns the working map of use cases, data flows, human review points, vendor/tool dependencies, government-contract constraints, and evidence trails?

The answer matters because “AI compliance” is rarely one document. It is usually a cross-functional operating record:

- what the system does,
- what data it uses,
- who reviews decisions,
- which contracts and customer requirements apply,
- what gets logged, tested, and preserved, and
- who can explain the record during financing, procurement, diligence, or a customer review.

For founders and GCs, the useful first step is not panic or a 60-page policy. It is a non-confidential inventory that turns scattered AI/autonomy facts into a readable workstream map.

I built the AI Compliance Gameplan Builder for that kind of first pass: `/ai-compliance-gameplan`
