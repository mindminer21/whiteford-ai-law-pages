## 6. Draft LinkedIn caption for Jeff review

The interesting physical-AI question is not “Can the robot work outside?”

It is “What gets smarter after the tenth jobsite?”

Gritt gave the market a useful public example this week. The construction-robotics company exited stealth with a $26 million Series A led by Obvious Ventures, bringing total funding to $32 million according to TechCrunch.

What matters for operators and investors is the architecture story behind the raise.

Gritt says it is not starting by building a custom robot fleet from scratch. It mounts intelligence on common equipment and off-the-shelf arms, beginning with solar-panel handling on real construction sites. The company reports field systems already working with major U.S. power construction customers, a contracted solar installation backlog measured in gigawatts, and a plan to expand from pick-and-place into more assembly and later infrastructure tasks.

Those are company-reported and press-reported claims—not a verdict on the company, the round, or the category.

But they surface a cleaner diligence agenda for physical AI.

Before a partner meeting or portfolio review, ask five questions:

1. **Unit of work**  
What exact job is being automated first—panel placement, material move, fastening, inspection or something else—and how is completion measured on a live site?

2. **Retrofit thesis**  
Why attach intelligence to equipment already on the job rather than ship a purpose-built robot first? What cost, deployment speed or service advantage does that create?

3. **Learning loop**  
After each deployment, what improves: perception, motion plans, exception handling, crew workflow, scheduling or inventory awareness? Who owns that feedback loop?

4. **Customer economic unit**  
What number does the buyer actually care about—crew output, schedule certainty, injury exposure, ability to staff remote sites, rework or total installed cost per unit?

5. **Second-task transfer**  
What is the next task that should reuse the same intelligence backbone without a ground-up rebuild? If the answer is fuzzy, the company may still be a clever demo rather than a platform.

If a team cannot answer those five crisply, more capital may buy more demos.

If they can, you are underwriting a learning system that compounds across messy outdoor work.

That is the difference between physical AI as theater and physical AI as an operating advantage.
