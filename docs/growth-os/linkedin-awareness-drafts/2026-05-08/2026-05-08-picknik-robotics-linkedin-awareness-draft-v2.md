Robotics is a useful place to see why “AI patent strategy” is not only an AI-company issue.

[Optional company tag only if Jeff manually verifies the exact LinkedIn company page and approves tagging: PickNik Robotics / PickNik AI]

PickNik's public materials describe MoveIt Pro as a platform for production-ready Physical AI and robotic manipulation applications. Their pages reference AI/ML-enabled capabilities, motion planning, computer vision, behavior trees, simulation/digital twins, open-source MoveIt foundations, and production-grade runtime tooling.

That mix raises a practical documentation point for technical leaders generally — not a company-specific legal conclusion and not a comment on any specific company's legal position:

When AI, ML, or advanced software helps a team move from prototype to deployable system, the patent/trade-secret conversation usually depends on the record.

A few non-confidential questions worth asking early:

- What did the human team actually conceive, select, improve, or decide?
- What did AI/ML tools help generate, rank, simulate, test, or automate?
- Which technical choices made the system work in the real world?
- Who contributed those choices — founders, employees, contractors, vendors, or open-source contributors?
- Which parts might be better candidates for patent review, and which might need trade-secret discipline instead?

This is not about assuming every robotics or AI-enabled company needs more patents. Sometimes the better next step is documentation cleanup, ownership review, confidentiality discipline, or waiting until the technical claim story is clearer.
