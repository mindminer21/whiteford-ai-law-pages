Deep-tech IP strategy usually gets harder right when the company gets more operational.

[Optional company tag only if Jeff manually verifies the exact LinkedIn company page and approves tagging: Electra]

Electra's public materials describe a clean-iron company moving through the kinds of milestones many advanced-technology teams recognize: patented technology, demonstration plant planning, partner testing and qualification, major financing, infrastructure scale-up, and a path toward commercial production.

That is a useful signal for a broader point — not a company-specific legal conclusion and not a comment on Electra's legal position:

For deep-tech, AI, data infrastructure, energy infrastructure, robotics, and advanced manufacturing companies, the IP question is rarely just “Do we have patents?”

A better operating question is:

Can the company connect the technical story, the invention record, the confidentiality discipline, the public claim story, and the commercialization roadmap before diligence pressure arrives?

A few non-confidential questions worth asking early:

- What technical choices made the process, model, system, or platform work?
- Who contributed those choices — founders, employees, contractors, vendors, university collaborators, partners, or customers?
- What has already been disclosed in public materials, demos, grant materials, investor materials, technical papers, or partner conversations?
- What belongs in a patent-review track, and what may be better protected through trade-secret controls?
- Do public technology claims line up with the documentation the company can actually show later?
- Are partner testing, qualification, customer pilots, and commercial-scale milestones creating records the company will need to explain in diligence?

This is not about assuming every technical milestone requires a patent filing.

Sometimes the better next step is invention-record cleanup, contributor mapping, confidentiality discipline, public-disclosure review, or a clearer patent-vs-trade-secret decision map.

For founders, CTOs, CFOs, GCs, and technical operators building AI-enabled or deep-tech products, we put together a short AI Patent Strategy Scorecard to help issue-spot the documentation questions before a fundraise, partner rollout, enterprise sale, public demo, or commercial-scale push:

https://whiteford-ai-law-pages.vercel.app/ai-patent-strategy-scorecard
