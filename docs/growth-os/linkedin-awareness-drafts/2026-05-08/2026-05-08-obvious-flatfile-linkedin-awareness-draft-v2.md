Why this company is a useful awareness example:

Obvious / Flatfile is a public-source example of a technology company whose materials sit at the intersection of AI-enabled data preparation, enterprise data workflows, customer implementation, data migration, mapping, validation, collaboration, and human review. Public materials describe:

- a company-level rebrand from Flatfile to Obvious while the Flatfile product remains unchanged, active, and supported;
- Flatfile as AI-powered data migration and preparation software for preparing, mapping, cleaning, transforming, and validating business-critical data;

## 6. Revised LinkedIn post — safer v2

Attorney-review-only draft — do not publish, schedule, tag, comment, DM, or repurpose externally without Jeff/reviewer approval.

AI in data products is not just a model question.

[Optional company tag only if Jeff manually verifies the exact LinkedIn company page and approves tagging: Obvious / Flatfile or Obvious (Flatfile product)]

Obvious / Flatfile is a useful public example of the broader trend. Flatfile's public materials describe AI-powered data migration and preparation software for enterprise workflows. The company's rebrand materials say Flatfile the company is now Obvious, while Flatfile the product remains active and supported.

That combination points to a practical operating issue for AI, SaaS, and data infrastructure companies generally — not a company-specific legal conclusion and not a comment on Obvious / Flatfile's legal position:

When AI is part of a data workflow, legal readiness usually depends on the workflow map, not just the policy memo.

A few non-confidential questions worth asking early:

- What customer or third-party data enters the product or implementation workflow?
- Where does AI assist with extraction, mapping, transformation, validation, analysis, or recommendations?
- Who reviews or approves AI-assisted changes before they affect a customer deliverable, migration, report, integration, or system of record?
- What customer contracts, security commitments, privacy terms, vendor terms, and internal policies govern that workflow?
- What records would explain the human decisions, AI-assisted steps, validation checks, and exception handling later?
- Do product, implementation, sales, security, customer success, finance, and legal share the same operating map?

This is not about turning every product decision into a legal project.

Sometimes the better next step is a lightweight use-case inventory, a data-flow map, a customer-commitment check, a vendor-term review, or clearer records of human review and approval.

For founders, CTOs, CFOs, GCs, and operators building AI-enabled data products, we put together a short Tech Company Legal Health Checklist to help issue-spot these operating-system gaps before an enterprise rollout, security review, customer implementation, financing, or diligence event:
