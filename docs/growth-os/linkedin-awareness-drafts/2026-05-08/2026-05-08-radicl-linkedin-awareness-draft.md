I was looking at public materials from RADICL, a Boulder cybersecurity company building around managed security operations for regulated-industry and defense-adjacent SMBs.

Useful category-wide issue for Colorado tech companies:

When a technical product starts selling trust, the legal operating system has to keep up with the product story.

That does not mean "call a lawyer because something is wrong."

It means founders and operators may want a clean map of the workstreams that start touching each other:

1. customer MSAs and security addenda,
2. vendor and subcontractor commitments,
3. employee and contractor IP ownership,
4. software / AI workflow ownership records,
5. privacy, security, and AI governance basics,
6. financing and board materials, and
7. diligence evidence that explains who approved what and why.

For technical companies selling into trust-sensitive markets, legal readiness can become part of go-to-market discipline.

Not as fear.

As operational clarity.

Educational starting point for Colorado / Mountain West tech teams: Colorado Tech Company Legal Health Checklist.
