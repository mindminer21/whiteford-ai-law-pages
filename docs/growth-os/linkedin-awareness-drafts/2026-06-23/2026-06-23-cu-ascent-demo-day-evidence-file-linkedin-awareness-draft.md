## 6. Draft LinkedIn post for Jeff’s manual review

Tomorrow’s CU Ascent Deep Tech Community Showcase is a good reminder:

The best demo-day follow-up usually is not a prettier deck.

It is a clean evidence file.

When a research-born company starts talking with investors, customers, strategic partners, or tech-transfer teams, the same questions appear fast:

1. What moved from lab result to customer use case?
2. Who contributed what — researcher, founder, engineer, advisor, contractor, model, or vendor?
3. What has already been disclosed publicly?
4. What is still nonpublic and should stay that way?
5. What evidence would survive a diligence request next week?

The practical founder artifact is a one-page “translation file”:

- technical thesis in business language
- proof points and dates
- contributor map
- ownership / assignment checklist
- public-disclosure log
- patent vs. trade-secret vs. know-how parking lot
- open questions for investors, customers, tech-transfer, and counsel

This is not about turning a startup into a legal memo.

It is about making the next conversation shorter, sharper, and less dependent on memory.

CU’s Ascent page describes deep-tech companies as often facing intensive R&D requirements, significant capital needs, and complex commercialization pathways. That is exactly why the operating record matters before the room gets busy.

If you are headed into a demo day, customer pilot, financing conversation, or university spinout meeting this week: build the file before the follow-up begins.

Optional resource if AI is part of the invention story: the free AI Patent Strategy Scorecard at learn.schellip.com is designed for non-confidential, categorical prep — not invention disclosures.
