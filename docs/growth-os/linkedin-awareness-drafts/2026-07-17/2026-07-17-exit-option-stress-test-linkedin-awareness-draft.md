## 6. Draft LinkedIn caption for Jeff review

An open exit market is not the same as an open exit path.

Crunchbase reported yesterday that Q2 produced the strongest venture-backed exit market since the 2021 boom, with both IPOs and startup acquisitions accelerating.

That is encouraging.

It can also tempt founder boards to ask the wrong first question:

“Should we pursue an IPO or a sale?”

For an in-house GC, CEO and CFO, the better first move is a two-column **exit-option stress test**.

Put “IPO” and “strategic sale” across the top. Then compare five questions:

1. **Outcome**  
What is the board actually optimizing for: liquidity, growth capital, strategic scale, founder transition, employee opportunity, customer reach or continued control?

2. **Timing**  
What must be true before this path is credible? Which market, performance, governance, buyer, financing or internal milestones control the clock?

3. **Business story**  
Can the same revenue, margin, customer, product and AI narrative withstand both public-investor scrutiny and a strategic buyer's integration questions?

4. **Dependencies**  
Which approvals, key agreements, customer commitments, ownership questions, people dependencies or third-party relationships could narrow one path faster than the other?

5. **Operating sequel**  
What must the company be able to do the day after the transaction: report publicly, integrate systems, retain key people, preserve customer trust, fund the roadmap or operate under a new control structure?

Mark each answer green, yellow or red for both paths.

Then assign one owner and one next decision to every yellow or red.

If the company can tell a coherent story only under one column, it may have an exit preference—but not yet exit optionality.

A stronger market should improve decision quality, not force a premature route choice.

For founders testing the M&A column, the free non-confidential Founder M&A Readiness Checklist is here: https://learn.schellip.com/founder-ma-readiness-checklist#checklist

Educational only; not legal, securities, tax, accounting, valuation or investment advice and not an attorney-client relationship.
