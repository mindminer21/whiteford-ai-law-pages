That mix raises a useful documentation point for technical leaders generally — not a company-specific legal conclusion:

When AI or advanced software helps a team move from prototype to deployable system, the patent/trade-secret conversation usually depends on the record.

A few non-confidential questions worth thinking about early:

- What did the human team actually conceive and decide?
- What did AI/ML tools help generate, rank, simulate, or test?
- Which technical choices made the system work in the real world?
- Who contributed those choices — founders, employees, contractors, vendors, open-source contributors?
- Which parts are better candidates for patent protection, and which may be better kept as trade secrets?

This is not about assuming every robotics company needs to file more patents. Sometimes the better answer is documentation, ownership cleanup, trade-secret discipline, or waiting until the technical claims are clearer.
