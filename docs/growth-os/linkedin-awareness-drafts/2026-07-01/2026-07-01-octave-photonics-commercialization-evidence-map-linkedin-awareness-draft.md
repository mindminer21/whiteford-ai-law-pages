> Shared infrastructure changes the startup checklist.
>
> OEDIT announced that Colorado Quantum Incubator, in partnership with Octave Photonics, was approved for a $500,000 grant to support Colorado’s first open-access photonic integrated circuit packaging and testing facility.
>
> That is a useful Colorado quantum/deep-tech signal.
>
> But for founders and CTOs, the practical lesson is bigger than the grant.
>
> When lab-originated technology starts moving through shared facilities, pilot programs, customer configurations, university/lab relationships, and investor conversations, the company needs more than a technical story.
>
> It needs a **commercialization evidence map**.
>
> Before the next customer pilot, board update, financing diligence, or strategic partnership conversation, I would want a simple record that answers:
>
> 1. **Technical edge** — what is the specific thing that works better, smaller, faster, cheaper, more reliably, or in a new environment?
>
> 2. **Proof trail** — what tests, prototypes, measurements, demos, facility runs, or customer observations support that story?
>
> 3. **Contributor map** — who contributed to the invention, design, software, process, data, packaging, or customer configuration?
>
> 4. **Ownership path** — what assignments, contractor terms, university/lab terms, facility rules, grant conditions, supplier terms, and customer pilot terms touch the work?
>
> 5. **Disclosure line** — what can be safely shown in a pitch deck, grant report, customer demo, paper, conference talk, or investor room without giving away the wrong thing?
>
> 6. **Product claim** — what can the company actually say the product does today, versus what is still a roadmap, research result, or lab demonstration?
>
> That map is not bureaucracy.
>
> It is how a technical company makes the jump from “we built something remarkable” to “customers, partners, and investors can trust the operating record behind it.”
>
> For AI-assisted R&D teams, I keep a free AI Patent Strategy Scorecard at learn.schellip.com/ai-patent-strategy-scorecard. But even if you never click it, the habit is the same:
>
> Build the record while the work is happening — not after diligence asks for it.
