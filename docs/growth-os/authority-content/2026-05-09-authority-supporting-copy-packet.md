### LinkedIn awareness draft — attorney review only

Post status: INTERNAL DRAFT ONLY. No publish, schedule, tag, comment, DM, boost, or manual posting authorization.

If AI helped your team build a new feature, the first patent-strategy question usually should not be limited to “Which tool did we use?”

A more useful starting point is whether the human technical contribution can be explained in a clean, non-confidential way:

- What problem did the team identify?
- What architecture, design choice, experiment, tuning, or product decision came from people?
- Where do the records live?
- Who contributed, and under what ownership or assignment path?
- Has anything been disclosed publicly already?

That early map does not answer patentability, inventorship, ownership, or filing strategy. But it can make a later patent-strategy review more focused.

For founders and CTOs using AI in R&D, the narrow diagnostic is simple: organize the human-contribution record before you ask whether the invention story is ready for a deeper review.

## Pillar 2 — AI Legal Risk and Compliance

Approved asset route: `/ai-compliance-gameplan`

Productized next step: AI Compliance Readiness Sprint
Matching authority backlog theme: AI governance readiness starts with an inventory before policy.

### LinkedIn awareness draft — attorney review only

Post status: INTERNAL DRAFT ONLY. No publish, schedule, tag, comment, DM, boost, or manual posting authorization.

A generic AI policy can look productive before the company has answered a simpler operating question:

Where is AI actually being used?

For many leadership teams, the first useful AI governance artifact is not a long policy. It is a practical use-case inventory:

- Use case and business owner
- Department or workflow
- Tool, vendor, or model category
- Data category involved
- How outputs are used
- Human review point
- Records location
- Escalation path

That inventory does not decide whether a company is compliant. It gives executives, legal, product, HR, finance, and operations a shared map for prioritizing review.

The goal is not panic. The goal is to see the real workflows before turning AI governance into policies, vendor reviews, training, and records.

Growing technology companies often do not need to “fix legal” all at once.

They need to know which legal operating workstream is most connected to the next business milestone.

A hiring push points to one set of questions. Enterprise sales points to another. Financing, AI/data expansion, product launch, strategic partnerships, and diligence each surface different legal operating needs.

A useful legal health checklist should help leadership sort work into practical categories:

- Contracts and customer/vendor process
- IP ownership and invention records
- Employment and contractor materials
- Privacy, security, and AI/data governance
- Corporate records and approvals
- Diligence readiness
- Owners, review dates, and escalation paths

The first output is not a legal conclusion. It is an operating map: what is scattered, stale, missing, or ownerless — and which category should be reviewed first.

### LinkedIn awareness draft — attorney review only

Post status: INTERNAL DRAFT ONLY. No publish, schedule, tag, comment, DM, boost, or manual posting authorization.

M&A readiness is not only a financial model.

For a technology company, avoidable diligence scramble can come from non-financial records that are hard to explain when a buyer, investor, or strategic partner starts asking detailed questions.

A practical pre-diligence map usually starts with categories like:

- Corporate records and approvals
- Cap table support
- IP ownership and invention assignments
- Contractor, vendor, and open-source records
- Customer and vendor contracts
- Employment materials
- Privacy, security, AI, and data governance
- Disputes or unresolved obligations
- Assignment, change-of-control, and consent questions

The narrow diagnostic is not “Will a deal happen?” or “What is the company worth?”

It is: which non-financial categories are organized, which are incomplete, and which may need legal review before diligence begins?
