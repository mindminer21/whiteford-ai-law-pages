## Official source-current anchor verification

### USPTO AI-assisted inventorship

Official anchors checked:

- USPTO revised inventorship guidance for AI-assisted inventions: https://www.uspto.gov/subscription-center/2025/revised-inventorship-guidance-ai-assisted-inventions
- USPTO 2024 AI-assisted inventorship guidance/examples: https://www.uspto.gov/subscription-center/2024/uspto-issues-inventorship-guidance-and-examples-ai-assisted-inventions
- USPTO FAQ: https://www.uspto.gov/initiatives/artificial-intelligence/faqs

Source-current findings:

- The 2025 USPTO page is live and titled `Revised inventorship guidance for AI-assisted inventions`; it states it was published 11/26/2025.
- The 2025 USPTO alert says the office published revised inventorship guidance for AI-assisted inventions and rescinded the February 2024 guidance in its entirety.
- The 2025 alert says longstanding precedent controls: the same legal standard for inventorship applies to all inventions, regardless of whether AI systems were used in the inventive process; no new, separate, or modified standard is created for AI-assisted inventions.
- The 2025 alert repeats the natural-person anchor: only natural persons can be properly named as inventors on patent applications; AI systems are tools used by human inventors and do not qualify for inventor status.
- The USPTO FAQ remains useful because it says the inventorship analysis focuses on the human contribution to conception and that the guidance does not create a heightened standard. It also says the guidance does not impose an additional duty to disclose AI use beyond existing USPTO rules/policies.

Recommended source wording for scripts:

> USPTO's current framing is not that AI-assisted inventions have a special inventorship standard. The current public USPTO materials emphasize the same inventorship standard, human contribution to conception, and the rule that inventors must be natural persons.

Avoid:

- Saying the 2024 guidance is the current controlling guidance without naming the 2025 revision/rescission.
- Saying AI use must always be disclosed to the USPTO as a separate AI-specific duty.
- Saying a viewer's invention is patentable, unpatentable, correctly inventored, or owned by a particular person/entity.

### Colorado SB25B-004 / SB26-189 / ADMT timing

Official anchors checked:

- Colorado SB25B-004, `Increase Transparency for Algorithmic Systems`: https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25b-004
- Colorado SB26-189, `Automated Decision-Making Technology`: https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189

Source-current findings:

- SB25B-004 page is live and states: `Concerning measures effective no later than June 30, 2026, to increase transparency for algorithmic systems.`
- SB25B-004 bill summary says SB24-205 created consumer protections in interactions with AI systems and that SB25B-004 extends the effective date of SB24-205 requirements to June 30, 2026.
- SB25B-004 status shown on the official page: `Became Law`; approved by Governor August 28, 2025; effective November 25, 2025.
- SB26-189 page is live and titled `Automated Decision-Making Technology`; it is a 2026 Regular Session bill concerning ADMT use in consequential decisions.
- SB26-189 summary says it repeals and reenacts SB24-205 provisions with new requirements regarding automated decision-making technology in consequential decisions.
- SB26-189 defines ADMT as technology that processes personal data and uses computation to generate outputs including predictions, recommendations, classifications, rankings, scores, or other information used to make/guide/assist a decision about an individual.
- SB26-189 defines consequential decisions around access, eligibility, or compensation related to education, employment, housing, financial or lending services, insurance, healthcare services, or essential government services.
- SB26-189 summary says developer duties for covered ADMT would start January 1, 2027, including technical documentation for intended uses, categories of training data, known limitations, and instructions for appropriate use and human review; developers and deployers would retain compliance records for at least 3 years.
- SB26-189 summary says the attorney general must adopt rules to clarify post-adverse-outcome disclosure requirements by January 1, 2027.
- The official SB26-189 page snapshot used for this note described the summary as applying to the reengrossed version introduced in the second house, so scripts should call it a bill/proposal/current legislative development unless attorney review confirms enactment status.

Recommended source wording for scripts:

> Colorado timing is moving. SB25B-004 became law and extended SB24-205 requirements to June 30, 2026. A separate 2026 ADMT bill, SB26-189, describes a more ADMT-specific framework and January 1, 2027 dates in its bill summary, but use bill-status language until counsel confirms the current enacted status.

Avoid:

- Treating SB26-189 as enacted final law without fresh attorney review.
- Telling viewers exactly whether their system is covered or compliant.
- Creating a generic `AI policy` video that skips the use-case inventory.

### Google video and helpful-content guidance

Official anchors checked:

- Google video structured data: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/video
- Google helpful, reliable, people-first content guidance: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

Source-current findings:

- Google says adding VideoObject structured data to watch pages can help Google find videos and influence details shown in video results, including description, thumbnail URL, upload date, and duration.
- Google recommends providing `contentUrl` where possible and `embedUrl` as an alternative; descriptions should be unique for each video.
- Google describes key moments as video segments/chapters that help users navigate a video; Google may detect them automatically, or publishers can provide markup.
- Google's helpful-content guidance says automated ranking systems prioritize helpful, reliable information created to benefit people, not content created to manipulate rankings.
- Google people-first guidance asks whether an intended audience would find the content useful, whether it demonstrates first-hand expertise/depth of knowledge, whether the site has a primary purpose, and whether readers leave feeling they learned enough to achieve their goal.
- Google warning signs include content primarily made to attract search-engine visits, broad content on many topics, summarizing others without adding much value, or writing about trending topics without audience fit.

Recommended production implications:

- Each video should have a distinct watch-page description, transcript/chapters, thumbnail, upload date, duration, and if self-hosted/embedded, VideoObject markup with appropriate `contentUrl`/`embedUrl` fields.
- Titles should be search-informed but not search-first. The video promise should be a concrete diagnostic outcome for founders/operators, not a broad legal explainer.
