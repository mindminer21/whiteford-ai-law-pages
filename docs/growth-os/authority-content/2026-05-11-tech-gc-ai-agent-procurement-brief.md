## 5. Useful insight / story structure

Use this as the backbone for a post, recording, newsletter segment, or internal review hub.

### A. Problem setup: the procurement meeting is too narrow

A product or operations team may ask: "Can this tool automate the workflow?" That is a useful question, but it is not the only one.

For a growing technology company, an AI-agent commitment can also raise questions about customer data, confidential information, output ownership, vendor training rights, auditability, indemnity, implementation responsibility, and whether the company's own customer promises still match the operational reality.

### B. The practical mistake: reviewing only the order form

The contract may be spread across an order form, master agreement, AI/product terms, data processing addendum, security exhibit, support terms, acceptable-use policy, privacy notice, model/vendor documentation, and renewal language. The operational risk is often in the gaps between those documents and the actual workflow.

### C. The useful frame: six gaps to map before commitment

1. IP and output-rights gap
   - Who can use prompts, outputs, automations, logs, workflow configurations, fine-tuning artifacts, connectors, feedback, and improvements?
   - Are vendor rights, company rights, customer restrictions, open-source terms, and internal invention records aligned?

2. Data-use and confidentiality gap
   - What data categories will move through the AI agent?
   - Are customer data, employee data, sensitive data, confidential information, credentials, source code, or regulated data excluded, permitted, retained, reviewed, or used for improvement/training?

3. Indemnity and liability gap
   - Do indemnity, warranty, limitation-of-liability, insurance, and remedy terms match the exposure created by customer-facing, employee-facing, product, or operational use?
   - Are AI-specific exclusions or usage restrictions visible to the business owner?

4. Audit, logging, and evidence gap
   - Can the company access usable logs, workflow histories, model/version records, admin events, approvals, and incident information if a customer, auditor, insurer, board member, or buyer asks?

5. Implementation and human-review gap
   - Who owns setup, approvals, fallback procedures, prompt/workflow changes, exception handling, and human review of outputs?
   - Are responsibilities documented outside the sales demo?

6. Renewal and change-control gap
   - What happens when features, sub-processors, models, data practices, pricing, support levels, or product terms change?
   - Who reviews renewals and expanded use before the AI agent becomes embedded in customer or internal workflows?

### D. Narrow next step: the 30-minute AI-agent procurement map

Before asking for a broad legal overhaul, make a one-page map:

- Vendor / tool name and intended workflow category
- Business owner and technical owner
- Data categories involved, using non-confidential labels only
- Customer, employee, product, finance, security, or legal touchpoints
- Contract documents collected
- Top two unknowns across IP/output, data use, indemnity/liability, audit/logging, implementation ownership, and change control
- Whether the issue belongs in vendor-contract review, privacy/security review, AI governance review, customer-contract alignment, or broader Tech GC planning

This is a checklist-style operating step, not a legal conclusion.
