## 3. Buyer diligence risk-map / top-gap language

Use “diligence friction,” “cleanup priority,” “risk map,” or “operating workstream” instead of definitive “deal risk” where possible. If “risk” appears in UI labels, define it as a triage signal rather than a legal conclusion.

### Risk-map intro copy

Your diligence friction map highlights categories where your answers suggest the most cleanup value may exist. These are not legal findings. Use them as a starting point for organizing records, assigning owners, and deciding where company-specific review may be needed.

### Category-specific modules

#### Corporate records

Top-gap label:

Corporate records and approvals

Copy:

The company may benefit from centralizing formation, equity, option/compensation, board/manager, consent, financing, and major-approval records before a diligence request arrives. A practical first step is to identify which records are current, which are stale, and who can validate the source file.

60-day action:

Days 1-20: Build a corporate-records index with owner, location, last update, and open questions for counsel review.

#### IP ownership

Top-gap label:

Ownership and assignment evidence

Copy:

The company may need a clearer way to show who created core product work and where founder, employee, contractor, advisor, vendor, open-source, and invention records live. This is often one of the first non-financial diligence areas to organize because it connects product value, deal story, and buyer counsel review.

60-day action:

Days 1-20: Inventory contributor and vendor records without adding confidential invention details to any public form.

#### Customer/vendor contracts

Top-gap label:

Key contract diligence

Copy:

The company may need a more organized view of key customer and vendor agreements, including assignability, change-of-control, termination, renewal, data/security, indemnity, unusual commitments, exclusivity, and customer-paper issues. The first move is usually an inventory and flagging exercise, not an immediate rewrite of every agreement.

60-day action:

Days 1-20 or 21-40: Create a key-contract tracker with owner, counterparty category, agreement type, renewal/termination dates, and review flags. Do not include confidential counterparty details in public-form fields.

#### Employment/contractor

Top-gap label:

Contributor and personnel documentation

Copy:

Hiring and contractor scale can create diligence friction if personnel files, contractor agreements, confidentiality language, assignment language, classification records, onboarding/offboarding materials, or advisor documentation are inconsistent or hard to locate.

60-day action:

Days 21-40: Build a contributor-record checklist for people and vendors who touched product, code, data, customer relationships, or material operations.

#### AI/data/privacy

Top-gap label:

AI, data, privacy, and product-claim evidence

Copy:

AI and data practices can affect contracts, confidentiality, IP ownership, privacy/security, customer claims, product documentation, and vendor terms. A useful diligence-prep step is to create a non-confidential map of what tools are used, what data categories are involved, who owns review, and what records support customer-facing statements.

60-day action:

Days 21-40: Assemble an AI/data/privacy evidence map with use-case category, owner, vendor category, contract status, notices/policies, security materials, and open review questions.

Branch note:

If AI/data/privacy is the dominant pressure point, the AI Compliance Gameplan Builder may be a useful separate diagnostic. Do not enroll the contact in that lane unless there is separate lane-specific opt-in or Jeff-approved compliant routing.

#### Disputes/concentration

Top-gap label:

Issue list and concentration map

Copy:

Known disputes, threatened claims, customer concentration, unusual obligations, exclusivity, non-standard commitments, or other exceptions may require careful counsel review before any external diligence sharing. The public checklist should not collect those facts. Internally, the company can prepare a non-privileged owner map and identify what needs privileged review.

60-day action:

Days 41-60: Prepare a non-confidential issue-category list, owner map, and counsel-review flag list without circulating privileged or sensitive details casually.

#### Diligence process

Top-gap label:

Data-room and request-list readiness

Copy:

The company may have records in place but no first-pass process for responding to buyer or investor diligence. A lightweight diligence folder and owner map can reduce scramble when the first request list arrives.

60-day action:

Days 41-60: Build a first-pass data-room checklist by category: corporate, equity, IP, contracts, employment/contractors, privacy/security, AI/data, disputes/exceptions, insurance, tax/finance support, and product/customer assurance materials.
