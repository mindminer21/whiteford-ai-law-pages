    11|    11|    11|    11|    11|## 2. LinkedIn post draft (900–1400 characters)

Colorado’s AI law timeline is shifting again, but the management question for executives is not “Do we wait?” It is “What should be ready before the deadline stops moving?”

For tech companies, the highest-leverage work is still practical:

• identify where AI is used in product, hiring, finance, support, security, and customer workflows
• separate ordinary productivity use from systems that may affect important decisions
• map vendors, data flows, contract terms, and human review points
• decide who owns intake, approval, escalation, documentation, and reporting

The companies that handle this well will not treat AI compliance as a policy PDF. They will build a lightweight legal operating system around AI use: inventory, owners, vendor review, documentation, and launch gates.

That posture matters for Colorado and Mountain West companies selling to enterprise customers. Buyers, investors, insurers, and strategic partners are already asking AI-risk questions while statutes and rules continue to develop.

This is not a call to overreact or assume every AI workflow is high risk. It is a call to stop letting AI adoption outrun the company’s ability to explain it.

Executive test: if a customer, board member, or acquirer asked tomorrow where AI is used and who approved it, could your team answer without a fire drill?
