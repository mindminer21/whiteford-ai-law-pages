## 1. Content Angle + Executive Hook

**Angle:** AI product risk is increasingly a diligence, procurement, financing, and board-readiness issue — not just a compliance memo issue. The companies that move fastest in transactions and enterprise reviews will be able to show a clean AI Diligence Room before a buyer, investor, customer, or insurer asks.

**Executive hook:** Before an AI-enabled technology company enters fundraising, enterprise procurement, strategic partnership, or M&A discussions, leadership should be able to produce a concise **AI Diligence Room**: system inventory, vendor/data map, IP ownership support, human-review evidence, and a prioritized cleanup plan.

## 2. LinkedIn Post Draft (900-1400 characters)

An AI feature can create business value quickly. It can also create diligence drag if the company cannot explain how the feature was built, sourced, governed, and owned.

That issue often appears before a sale process. It shows up in enterprise procurement, financing, board review, insurance renewal, customer security review, or a strategic partnership.

For Colorado and Mountain West technology companies, a practical next step is to build an AI Diligence Room before anyone asks for it:

• AI systems and vendors in use, including embedded tools
• what data goes in, what outputs come out, and who relies on them
• IP ownership, invention-assignment, contractor, customer, and vendor terms
• human review, escalation, and recordkeeping for higher-risk uses
• open questions for product, legal, security, HR, and finance

This is not about predicting every future law or turning product teams into lawyers. It is about reducing surprise.

When a buyer, investor, enterprise customer, or board asks whether the company can stand behind its AI product story, leadership should not have to reconstruct the answer from Slack threads, vendor demos, and half-remembered procurement decisions.

A short diligence-readiness file can turn AI risk from a vague concern into a managed workstream.

## 3. Short Email / Newsletter Draft

**Subject:** Build the AI diligence room before a buyer asks  
**Preview text:** AI product value can become diligence friction if ownership, vendor, data, and review records are scattered.

**Body:**

AI features are moving from experiments into customer-facing products, internal workflows, HR tools, security reviews, and transaction conversations.

The legal problem is not simply that a company uses AI. The operational problem is that the company may not be able to explain the AI product story when someone important asks: a buyer, investor, enterprise customer, board member, insurer, or regulator.

A useful executive starting point is an **AI Diligence Room**:

- the AI systems and vendors in use;
- the data inputs, outputs, and business reliance points;
- IP ownership support across employees, contractors, customers, and vendors;
- product claims, human-review paths, escalation, and recordkeeping;
- open cleanup items across legal, product, security, HR, finance, and procurement.

This is not a legal conclusion about compliance, patentability, inventorship, or deal risk. It is a practical readiness file that helps leadership identify where scattered AI decisions may create friction before timing becomes urgent.

## 7. Source Links / Research Anchors

1. USPTO — Revised inventorship guidance for AI-assisted inventions:  
   https://www.uspto.gov/subscription-center/2025/revised-inventorship-guidance-ai-assisted-inventions  
   Safe use: USPTO states that AI systems, including generative AI and other computational models, are tools used by human inventors and do not qualify for inventor status. Use this to support careful documentation of human contribution and tool role; do not imply a definitive inventorship conclusion.

2. NIST — AI Risk Management Framework:  
   https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework  
   Safe use: NIST provides non-binding governance vocabulary for mapping, measuring, managing, and governing AI risk. Use it as a practical framework anchor, not as a standalone legal requirement.
