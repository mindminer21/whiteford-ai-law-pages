## 3. Core pain and desirable outcome

### Core pain

AI tools enter the company through normal operating decisions: a sales meeting summarizer, product model experiment, coding assistant, customer-support tool, HR screening vendor, finance forecaster, cybersecurity platform, or data workflow. Each may be reasonable on its own, but the executive problem is that no one can quickly answer:

- Which AI tools are approved, piloted, tolerated, or prohibited?
- Who owns each tool or use case?
- What generic category of data or output is involved?
- Which vendor terms govern confidentiality, model training, output rights, audit, security, indemnity, and customer-flowdown questions?
- Which uses should pause for legal review before launch, especially if they may affect employment, lending, housing, health-care, insurance, legal-service, or other consequential-decision contexts?
- What evidence gap will matter at the next product, customer, investor, board, diligence, or compliance conversation?

### Desirable outcome

A calm, non-confidential **AI Vendor Intake Snapshot** that gives leadership a first-pass map of tools, owners, data categories, contract status, review triggers, and next business events — enough to prepare better questions for counsel and decide whether the company needs a broader `/ai-compliance-gameplan` or `/colorado-tech-general-counsel` conversation.

This is not a compliance determination. It is a narrow diagnostic first step.
