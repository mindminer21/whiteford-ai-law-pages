# Google / YouTube SEO Opportunity Research — 2026-07-20

Internal research only. Search results, autocomplete, and view counts are a dated snapshot, not legal advice, publication approval, or a forecast of search volume.

## Executive recommendation

Record in this order:

1. **Colorado AI law after July 13: the 2027 ADMT + chatbot rules**
2. **AI-assisted invention: who conceived the claimed invention?**
3. **Buying an AI startup: can the buyer legally and operationally use what it is buying?**

Use generic `outside general counsel`, `fractional general counsel`, and `startup M&A readiness` language as conversion/support terms, not the lead search promise. The stronger discovery phrases are concrete questions about effective dates, inventorship, AI-agent risks, and AI-target diligence.

## Method and terminology

Research was performed July 20, 2026 (MDT) using:

- Google autocomplete queries from Google's public suggestion endpoint.
- YouTube autocomplete queries from the same suggestion service with the YouTube surface selected.
- Current YouTube search snapshots collected with `yt-dlp` for the topic clusters below.
- Current Google/web result snapshots from Firecrawl search.
- Official source checks against the Colorado Attorney General, Colorado General Assembly, and USPTO.

**Demand texture, not volume:** autocomplete means a phrase or close variant was suggested on the surface at the time checked. It is directional evidence about wording and intent, not monthly search volume, traffic potential, ranking difficulty, or conversion rate.

**YouTube gap:** a gap means the current result set was stale, generic, weakly matched, dominated by a different intent, or had few clear operator/founder explainers. It is not proof that a video will rank or attract a particular number of views.

## Current YouTube result evidence

These are examples from the July 20 snapshot, included to show result shape rather than to estimate demand:

- **AI-assisted inventions patent law:** the exact search returned founder-facing videos such as `Did The Patent Office Just Change Everything For AI?` (427 views), `Can I Get a Patent for an A.I.-Assisted Invention?` (13 views), and several hour-long panels. The result set has little concise treatment of the USPTO's November 2025 rescission/reset.
- **Colorado AI Act compliance:** results included a CBS Colorado legislative recap (3,030 views), a broad ServiceNow regulation explainer (466 views), older SB24-205 compliance content, and several 2026 preliminary-analysis videos with single-digit views. `Colorado ADMT rulemaking` returned one law-firm webinar (21 views) and one multi-state webinar (82 views).
- **Colorado chatbot safety act:** the exact search returned one Colorado Public Radio segment (38 views), one global regulatory roundup (48 views), and no clear operator-focused walkthrough of the enacted Colorado requirements.
- **AI M&A due diligence:** results were dominated by AI-assisted review tools and workflow demos. Current target-side legal videos about training-data rights, foundation-model/API continuity, AI-specific warranties, and post-close usability were scarce.
- **Outside general counsel technology startup:** results were sparse, interview-led, and generally low-view. This reinforces using OGC as the destination, not the discovery hook.

## Top three recording topics

### 1. Colorado AI Law Changed Again: ADMT + Chatbot Rules for 2027

**Why now**

- Google demand texture contains `summary`, `effective date`, `update`, `repealed`, `delayed`, `2026`, employment, ADMT, and chatbot-law variants.
- The Colorado Attorney General page was updated after the July 13 pre-rulemaking comment window and now covers both SB26-189 and HB26-1263.
- YouTube has almost no clear post-comment-window explanation tying the two laws together for Colorado operators.

**Primary title**

`Colorado AI Law Changed Again: ADMT + Chatbot Rules for 2027`

**Alternate titles**

- `Colorado AI Compliance After July 13: What Companies Should Do Next`
- `Colorado's 2027 AI Rules: ADMT, Chatbots, and the Questions to Ask Now`

**Thumbnail pattern**

- Text: `2 LAWS • 1/1/27`
- Visual: Jeff on one side; simple Colorado outline split into `ADMT` and `CHATBOTS`; high-contrast navy/white with one Colorado-red accent.
- Avoid screenshots of statutes, binders, folders, checklists, or document stacks.

**Search phrase package**

Primary: `Colorado AI Act update`  
Secondary: `Colorado AI Act effective date`, `Colorado AI compliance`, `Colorado ADMT law`, `Colorado chatbot law`, `Colorado AI Act employment`

**Answer promise**

Distinguish what is enacted, what takes effect January 1, 2027, what is still awaiting formal rules, and what business facts should be identified now. Do not present unresolved rulemaking questions as final requirements.

### 2. AI Helped Invent It—Can You Still Patent It?

**Why now**

- Exact Google phrasing is strong and specific: `ai assisted inventions patent law`, `ai assisted inventions uspto`, `patenting ai assisted inventions`, and `can ai be a patent inventor`.
- The USPTO's November 26, 2025 guidance rescinded the February 2024 guidance, applies the ordinary inventorship standard, and limits named inventors to natural persons.
- A July 16, 2026 Reuters legal column on AI-driven drug discovery provides a fresh news peg, while YouTube remains dominated by old guidance, practitioner panels, and AI drafting/tool content.

**Primary title**

`AI Helped Invent It—Can You Still Patent It? (USPTO 2026)`

**Alternate titles**

- `AI-Assisted Inventions: Who Counts as the Inventor Now?`
- `The USPTO Reset Its AI Inventorship Guidance—What Founders Need to Know`

**Thumbnail pattern**

- Text: `WHO CONCEIVED IT?`
- Visual: Jeff plus a clean human/AI split around one lightbulb or claim-feature diagram; one accent arrow points to the human side.
- Avoid robot-gavel clichés and dense patent-document imagery.

**Search phrase package**

Primary: `AI-assisted inventions patent law`  
Secondary: `AI-assisted inventions USPTO`, `patenting AI-assisted inventions`, `can AI be a patent inventor`, `USPTO guidance AI-assisted inventions`

**Answer promise**

Explain the current natural-person and conception baseline, then give a scenario showing the difference between using AI as a tool and identifying the human contribution to claimed subject matter. Keep patentability, ownership, eligibility, and inventorship analytically separate.

### 3. Buying an AI Startup? 7 Legal Questions Before the LOI

**Why now**

- Google demand texture supports `ai due diligence m&a`, `m&a technology due diligence checklist`, `technology due diligence m&a`, and `ai startup acquisitions`.
- Current 2026 legal commentary emphasizes training-data rights, model/API dependencies, technical testing, AI-specific warranties, and whether an acquired AI system remains usable at enterprise scale.
- YouTube's AI M&A results mostly cover AI tools that perform diligence, not diligence of the AI target itself.

**Primary title**

`Buying an AI Startup? 7 Legal Questions Before the LOI`

**Alternate titles**

- `AI M&A Due Diligence: Can You Use What You're Buying?`
- `Before You Acquire an AI Company, Test These 7 Assumptions`

**Thumbnail pattern**

- Text: `CAN YOU USE IT?`
- Visual: Jeff plus a simple acquisition arrow crossing five labeled dependencies: `DATA`, `MODEL`, `API`, `IP`, `RULES`; use one red break mark on the weakest link.
- Do not use a virtual room, folder tree, document pile, binder, or branded “proof” metaphor.

**Search phrase package**

Primary: `AI M&A due diligence`  
Secondary: `AI startup acquisition`, `M&A technology due diligence`, `AI startup due diligence`, `buying an AI startup`, `AI due diligence for acquirers`

**Answer promise**

Show how a buyer tests data rights, model and API continuity, open-source/foundation-model terms, human and contractor IP ownership, regulatory exposure, enterprise claims, and post-close integration assumptions. Keep the focus on value and usability rather than document organization.

## Title and thumbnail patterns to standardize

### Title patterns

1. **Consequential question + exact topic**  
   `AI Helped Invent It—Can You Still Patent It?`
2. **Current change + audience implication**  
   `Colorado AI Law Changed Again: What Companies Need for 2027`
3. **Stakeholder + decision point + bounded number**  
   `Buying an AI Startup? 7 Legal Questions Before the LOI`
4. **Action verb + high-stakes object**  
   `Before an AI Agent Can Buy, Send, or Approve—Who Authorized It?`

Put the natural-language question first. Place the formal keyword, agency, statute, or year after the colon or in the parenthetical. Do not lead with firm-service terminology.

### Thumbnail patterns

The clearest current legal/business thumbnails use:

- One human face, generally taking 30–45% of the frame.
- Two to five large words; six only when unavoidable.
- One dominant contrast pair (navy/white, black/yellow, or blue/white) and one accent color.
- One visual idea: Colorado + two law labels; human/AI + conception; acquisition arrow + dependencies.
- A hierarchy that reads at mobile size: question first, face second, one icon/diagram third.
- Thumbnail copy that complements rather than repeats the title.

Avoid tiny subtitles, multiple headshots, generic courthouse/gavel imagery, screenshots, and any folder, binder, document-stack, transaction-room, proof, or dossier metaphor.

## Practical SEO packaging

For each video:

1. Use the primary phrase naturally in the first 120 characters of the description.
2. Put a 40–60 word direct answer immediately below the opening sentence for extractability.
3. Add chapters named as natural questions, not abstract section labels.
4. Publish a companion page with the same answer promise, official source links, author credentials, and a visible `Last updated: July 2026` date.
5. Include one precise CTA matched to the decision: compliance gameplan, AI patent strategy scorecard, tech-GC conversation, or transaction counsel route.
6. Recheck official sources on recording/publication day; do not imply final Colorado rules before they are proposed/adopted.
