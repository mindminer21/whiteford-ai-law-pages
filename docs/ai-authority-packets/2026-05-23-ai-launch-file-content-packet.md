## 2. LinkedIn Post Draft (900–1400 characters)

AI product launches are starting to look less like software releases and more like executive control events.

Before an AI feature reaches customers, procurement, investors, or a diligence room, leadership should be able to answer one practical question: where is the launch file?

Not a 70-page policy. A working file that shows:

• what the AI system does and who owns it
• which vendors, models, and datasets are involved
• where customer, employee, or sensitive data can flow
• what the contract says about confidentiality, IP ownership, audit, security, and indemnity
• whether human review exists for high-impact decisions
• what evidence the company will keep if a buyer, board, or enterprise customer asks later

Colorado’s AI law timeline has moved, and details matter. But the durable lesson for Mountain West technology companies is operational: AI risk cannot live only in a policy. It has to connect product, engineering, HR, procurement, contracts, IP, privacy, and board reporting.

That is outside-GC work as much as compliance work.

The opportunity is to make AI launch readiness a repeatable legal operating system: clear enough for executives, disciplined enough for diligence, and practical enough that product teams will use it.

## 3. Short Email / Newsletter Draft

**Subject:** Where is the AI launch file?

**Preview text:** A practical outside-GC frame for AI product, vendor, data, IP, and diligence readiness.

**Body:**

Executives do not need every AI issue reduced to a legal memo. They need a working launch file the company can use before an AI feature reaches customers, enterprise procurement, investors, employees, or diligence.

A useful AI launch file answers seven operating questions:

1. What does the AI system do, and who owns it internally?
2. Which vendors, models, datasets, and integrations are involved?
3. What data can enter or leave the system?
4. What contract terms control confidentiality, IP ownership, security, audit rights, indemnity, and downstream use?
5. Where is human review required for consequential decisions?
6. What evidence will the company keep for board, buyer, customer, or regulator questions?
7. Which legal workstream owns the next 30 days?

Colorado’s AI rules continue to evolve, and national frameworks such as the NIST AI RMF are not a substitute for legal analysis. But they do point to the same executive habit: map the system, assign owners, document decisions, and manage risk as part of the product lifecycle.

For a technology company, this is the legal operating layer between invention, launch, contracts, employment, privacy, and transaction readiness.
