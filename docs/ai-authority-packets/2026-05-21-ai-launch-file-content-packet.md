## 2. LinkedIn post draft

AI features are moving from experiment to operating layer.

That changes the legal question for leadership. It is no longer only: “Can we use this tool?” It is also: “Can we explain, govern, contract around, and diligence this use later?”

For many technology companies, the answer is an AI Launch File before an AI-enabled feature reaches customers, procurement, investors, or a buyer’s diligence team.

A launch file answers six questions:

• What does the system do, and who owns it internally?
• What data is used, retained, shared, or restricted?
• Which vendor terms control confidentiality, IP, security, audit, and indemnity?
• Where do human review, escalation, notices, and explanations live?
• What invention, authorship, trade secret, and product evidence should be preserved?
• What changes if the use case touches employment, credit, housing, insurance, health, education, or another sensitive decision area?

This is not about slowing the business down. It is about giving leadership a clean operating layer: product can ship with fewer surprises; sales can answer procurement; finance can prepare for diligence; HR and risk can see review points; legal can prioritize instead of react.

For Colorado and Mountain West technology companies, AI readiness is now outside-general-counsel work.

## 3. Short email/newsletter draft

**Subject:** Before the AI feature ships, build the launch file  
**Preview text:** A practical legal operating layer for AI product, vendor, IP, and diligence risk.

**Body:**

AI legal readiness is easiest to manage before an AI feature becomes part of the product, customer promise, vendor stack, or diligence story.

A practical step for technology-company leadership is an **AI Launch File**: a short, maintained record that connects the AI use case to the company’s legal operating system.

At minimum, the file should answer:

- what the AI system does and who owns it internally;
- what data, vendors, and customer-facing claims are involved;
- which contract terms control confidentiality, IP ownership, indemnity, audit, and security;
- where human review, escalation, notices, and records live;
- what invention, authorship, trade secret, and product evidence should be preserved; and
- whether the use case creates heightened sensitivity under employment, consumer, financial, education, health, housing, insurance, or similar decision contexts.

This is not a legal conclusion or a substitute for advice on a specific launch. It is a way for executives to see whether the company has a repeatable system instead of a collection of one-off AI decisions.
