## Executive recommendation

Primary topic for the 2026-05-14 Daily Whiteford AI Authority Packet:

**AI-assisted invention records: the USPTO’s current standard is not “AI means no patent”; it is “identify and document the human inventive contribution.”**

Why this is the safest primary angle today:

1. It avoids repeating the 2026-05-13 Colorado AI delay/readiness packet as the lead topic.
2. It is grounded in current official USPTO/Federal Register materials, including the 2025 revised guidance replacing the 2024 AI-assisted inventorship guidance.
3. It maps cleanly to the existing `/ai-patent-strategy-scorecard` route and `ai-patent` campaign key.
4. It gives prospects one narrow diagnostic before any consultation: “Can your team explain who contributed what, what the AI did, and what records support the invention story?”
5. It is useful for AI, robotics, SaaS, autonomy, cyber, data, and deep-tech founders without requiring live-source prospecting or confidential facts.

Recommended packet hook:

> If your team used AI during R&D, the first patent-strategy question is not whether AI touched the work. It is whether the human contribution to conception is clear enough to explain, document, and align with the filing strategy.

## Source and demand evidence

### Official/current legal anchors

1. USPTO AI-related resources
   - URL: https://www.uspto.gov/initiatives/artificial-intelligence/artificial-intelligence-resources
   - Current useful point: USPTO describes this as a list of current guidance and training materials relevant to AI. The page states that, in November 2025, USPTO issued new guidance clarifying that the same legal standard for determining inventorship applies to all inventions, regardless of whether AI systems were used in the inventive process, and that there is no separate or modified standard for AI-assisted inventions.

2. Federal Register API record: Revised Inventorship Guidance for AI-Assisted Inventions
   - URL: https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2025-21457.json
   - HTML URL: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/28/2025-21457/revised-inventorship-guidance-for-ai-assisted-inventions
   - Raw text URL: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2025/11/28/2025-21457.txt
   - Current useful point: The 2025 notice states that USPTO rescinds and replaces the previously published February 2024 inventorship guidance. In the text, USPTO states: “The same legal standard for determining inventorship applies to all inventions, regardless of whether AI systems were used in the inventive process” and “There is no separate or modified standard for AI-assisted inventions.” It also notes AI systems are not persons and cannot be joint inventors.

3. Federal Register API record: 2024 Inventorship Guidance for AI-Assisted Inventions
   - URL: https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2024-02623.json
   - HTML URL: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/02/13/2024-02623/inventorship-guidance-for-ai-assisted-inventions
   - Raw text URL: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2024/02/13/2024-02623.txt
   - Use only as history/context because it was replaced by 2025 guidance. Useful historic wording: AI-assisted inventions were not described as categorically unpatentable, and analysis focused on human contributions.

4. Google Search Central: Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content
   - URL: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content
   - Current useful point: Google recommends content created primarily for people, with helpful summaries and useful explanations, not search-engine-first content. This supports a practical executive diagnostic rather than generic “AI patent law” hype.

5. Google Search Central: Google Search’s guidance about AI-generated content
   - URL: https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/02/google-search-and-ai-content
   - Current useful point: Google says helpfulness, originality, quality, and E-E-A-T matter regardless of whether AI is used in production. Use this to keep the packet focused on Jeff’s legal/IP experience and concrete founder questions.

6. YouTube Help: YouTube Search & Discovery performance FAQ
