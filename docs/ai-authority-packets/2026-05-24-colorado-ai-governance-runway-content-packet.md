## 2. LinkedIn post draft

Colorado’s AI Act runway is useful only if leadership turns it into evidence.

SB25B-004 extended the effective date of SB24-205 requirements to June 30, 2026. That may feel like extra time. For companies using AI in hiring, benefits, customer support, fraud review, pricing, or other consequential workflows, the better read is: you now have a window to build the operating layer before a regulator, enterprise customer, insurer, investor, or buyer asks for it.

The first move is not a 40-page policy. It is an AI decision inventory:

• What AI systems are in use, including embedded vendor tools?
• Which use cases affect people, employees, applicants, or customers?
• Who owns each system internally?
• What data, model output, human review, notice, logging, and vendor terms support the workflow?
• What records would show reasonable governance if the system is challenged?

That work is not just “compliance.” It also improves procurement, product launch discipline, HR risk management, diligence readiness, and board reporting.

The companies that win with AI will not be the ones waiting for perfect legal certainty. They will be the ones that can explain how AI is being used, who is accountable, and what evidence supports the controls.

## 3. Short email / newsletter draft

**Subject:** Colorado AI readiness: the inventory before the policy

**Preview text:** The June 30, 2026 runway is an opportunity to build evidence, not wait for perfect certainty.

**Body:**

Colorado’s AI Act timeline has shifted, but the underlying business question has not: if an enterprise customer, board member, investor, insurer, employee, regulator, or buyer asked how your company uses AI in consequential workflows, could leadership answer with evidence?

For many companies, the next useful step is not a comprehensive policy. It is a practical AI decision inventory:

1. AI systems and embedded vendor tools in use.
2. Use cases that may affect people, employees, applicants, customers, or regulated decisions.
3. Internal owners for each workflow.
4. Vendor contract terms touching data, confidentiality, IP rights, audit, security, indemnity, and output use.
5. Human review, notice, logging, escalation, and recordkeeping practices.

That inventory becomes the foundation for a Colorado AI readiness plan, vendor-risk review, HR/product governance, and diligence-ready documentation. It also keeps the legal work tied to operating decisions instead of abstract policy language.

This is general information and not legal advice. The right next step depends on the company’s systems, uses, contracts, and risk profile.
