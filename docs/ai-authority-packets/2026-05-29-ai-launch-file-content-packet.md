## 2. LinkedIn post draft — 1,305 characters

AI product launches are starting to fail a different kind of diligence test.

Not “does the model work?”

“Can the company explain what the system does, who owns it, what data and vendors touch it, what promises sales is making, and where human review sits?”

That is why I like the discipline of an AI Launch File.

Before an AI feature reaches customers, enterprise procurement, investors, or buyer diligence, leadership should be able to point to a compact record:

1. use case + accountable owner
2. data/model path and retention limits
3. vendor terms for confidentiality, IP, audit, security, and indemnity
4. human review and escalation for higher-risk outputs
5. invention, authorship, assignment, and trade-secret evidence
6. sensitive-decision screen for employment, credit, housing, insurance, health, education, or similar exposure

This is not bureaucracy for its own sake. It is how product, sales, finance, HR/risk, and legal stop answering AI questions from memory.

The law is still moving, including in Colorado. But the operating questions are already here.

If your team is moving AI from experiment to revenue, start with the file you would want to hand to a board, customer, investor, or acquirer.

Draft educational note only — not legal advice.

## 3. Short email / newsletter draft

**Subject:** The AI launch file your board will eventually ask for

**Preview text:** Before AI moves from experiment to revenue, create the record your customers, investors, and buyers will expect.

**Body:**

AI readiness is often framed as a policy problem. For executives, it is also an operating-record problem.

Before an AI feature reaches customers, enterprise procurement, investors, or buyer diligence, the company should be able to answer a short set of practical questions:

- What does the system do, and who owns the internal record?
- What data, model, and vendor path supports it?
- What do the vendor contracts say about confidentiality, IP ownership, security, audit rights, and indemnity?
- Where does human review or escalation occur?
- What invention, authorship, assignment, and trade-secret evidence exists?
- Could the use case touch employment, credit, housing, insurance, education, health, or another sensitive-decision setting?

That file will not answer every legal question. It will, however, make the next legal conversation faster and more concrete.

If your team is commercializing AI, the right first step is usually not a long policy memo. It is a focused legal-health review tied to the next business milestone: product launch, enterprise sale, financing, or diligence.
