## 2) AI Patent Brief — “Human Contribution After the USPTO Reset”

- **Primary keyword:** `AI-assisted inventions patent law`
- **Search intent:** A founder or inventor used AI in R&D and wants to understand the current USPTO baseline and what internal evidence may help counsel evaluate inventorship, ownership, patent, and trade-secret strategy.
- **Title options:**
  1. `AI Helped Invent It—What Does the USPTO Require Now?`
  2. `AI-Assisted Inventions After the USPTO’s 2025 Inventorship Reset`
  3. `AI-Assisted R&D: The Inventorship Questions to Ask Before Filing`
- **Thumbnail hook:** `WHO CONCEIVED THE CLAIMED INVENTION?`
- **6-part outline:**
  1. Cold open: “The current USPTO question is not whether AI touched the project; it is who actually conceived the claimed invention.”
  2. Source-current baseline: the November 26, 2025 USPTO guidance rescinded the February 2024 guidance; only natural persons may be named inventors.
  3. No special AI-only test: the USPTO says the same inventorship standard applies whether or not AI tools were used.
  4. Facts counsel may need—not a USPTO checklist: problem framing, human design choices, prompts/instructions, architecture decisions, experiments, rejected paths, prototypes, claim-feature mapping, and dated contributors.
  5. Ownership/strategy fork: employee and contractor assignments, vendor/model terms, collaborations, confidentiality, patent versus trade secret, and product-roadmap timing.
  6. CTA: run the scorecard without disclosing confidential invention details, then bring the resulting issue map to counsel.
- **Cited source anchors:**
  - USPTO revised inventorship guidance announcement: https://www.uspto.gov/subscription-center/2025/revised-inventorship-guidance-ai-assisted-inventions
  - Federal Register revised guidance, 90 FR 54636: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/28/2025-21457/revised-inventorship-guidance-for-ai-assisted-inventions
  - USPTO AI resources: https://www.uspto.gov/initiatives/artificial-intelligence/artificial-intelligence-resources

## 3) Founder Legal Operating System — “Who Can the AI Agent Act For?”

- **Primary keyword:** `AI governance checklist`
- **Search intent:** A founder, CTO, GC, product leader, buyer, or investor wants concrete authority, approval, and escalation questions now that AI agents can access systems and take actions—not merely generate text.
- **Title options:**
  1. `Before AI Agents Touch Company Systems, Answer These Authority Questions`
  2. `The AI Governance Checklist for Agents That Can Actually Take Action`
  3. `AI Agents in Enterprise Systems: Authority, Approval, and Escalation`
- **Thumbnail hook:** `WHO CAN YOUR AI AGENT ACT FOR?`
- **6-part outline:**
  1. Cold open: “Once AI can send, book, buy, approve, change code, or move data, a policy alone does not answer who authorized the action or who can stop it.”
  2. Agent roster: name, business owner, purpose, vendor/model, environments, connected systems, and data categories.
  3. Identity and authorization: which person or service account authorizes the agent, permission scope, action thresholds, approval gates, segregation of duties, and revocation path.
  4. Action evidence: task/source trail, confirmation, logs, exceptions, rollback/recovery, incident owner, and retention location.
  5. Rights and promises: vendor terms, model/data use, confidentiality, IP ownership, customer commitments, privacy/security statements, and supported product claims.
  6. Enterprise and transaction questions: decide who can explain the agent roster, permissions, contract terms, action history, exceptions, and remediation; CTA to the Tech Company Legal Health Checklist.
- **Cited source anchors:**
  - NIST AI Agent Standards Initiative (voluntary standards work): https://www.nist.gov/artificial-intelligence/ai-agent-standards-initiative
  - NIST/NCCoE Software and AI Agent Identity and Authorization project (concept phase): https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/projects/software-and-ai-agent-identity-and-authorization
  - FTC on AI privacy and confidentiality commitments: https://www.ftc.gov/policy/advocacy-research/tech-at-ftc/2024/01/ai-companies-uphold-your-privacy-confidentiality-commitments
  - FTC on deceptive AI claims: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes
