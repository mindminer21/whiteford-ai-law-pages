## 2. Polished LinkedIn post \(900–1400 characters\)

AI legal readiness often starts with a surprisingly ordinary object: a use register.

Not a 40-page policy. Not a board deck. A working list that lets leadership see where AI is actually being used across the company.

For a Colorado or Mountain West technology company, that register should usually answer seven practical questions:

• What is the AI use case or AI-enabled feature?
• Who owns it inside the business?
• What data flows into the tool?
• Does the output affect customers, employees, investors, or regulated decisions?
• What vendor terms govern confidentiality, IP ownership, security, indemnity, audit, and data use?
• What human review or escalation path exists?
• What evidence would the company produce in procurement, financing, diligence, or a board-level risk review?

This is not about freezing useful AI adoption while the law continues to develop. It is about making AI use visible enough that product, finance, HR, risk, and legal can govern it.

The companies that will handle AI legal risk best are not necessarily the ones with the longest policy. They are the ones that can quickly show what is being used, who is accountable, which contracts control it, and where human judgment remains in the loop.

## 3. Short email / newsletter draft

**Subject:** Before the AI policy, build the AI use register  
**Preview text:** Make AI use visible before launch, renewal, financing, or diligence.

**Body:**

Most technology companies now have AI in more than one place: employee tools, vendor products, internal workflows, product features, and sometimes customer-facing decisions.

The legal problem is often not that every use is obviously high-risk. The problem is visibility.

A simple AI use register gives leadership a shared map:

- the use case and internal owner;
- the data flowing into the tool;
- the vendor terms that control confidentiality, IP, security, audit, indemnity, and data use;
- the human-review or escalation path;
- whether the use could affect customers, employees, investors, regulated decisions, or external product claims; and
- what evidence the company could produce in procurement, financing, diligence, or a board-level risk review.

That register will not answer every legal question. But it gives counsel and management a practical place to start — and it avoids treating AI adoption as invisible until a customer, investor, buyer, or regulator asks for the record.
