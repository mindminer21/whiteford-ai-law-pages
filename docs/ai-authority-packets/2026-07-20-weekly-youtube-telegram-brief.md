## 2) AI Patent Brief — Before You Put an Invention Into an AI Drafting Tool

- **Primary keyword:** `AI patent drafting`
- **Search intent:** A technical founder or inventor is considering an AI drafting/search tool and wants to protect confidential technical information while preserving sound inventorship, ownership, filing, and claim-strategy decisions.
- **Title options:**
  1. `Before You Put an Invention Into an AI Patent Drafting Tool`
  2. `AI Patent Drafting: 6 Questions Founders Should Ask First`
  3. `Using AI for a Patent Application? Start With Confidentiality and Human Review`
- **Thumbnail hook:** `DON’T PASTE THE INVENTION YET`
- **6-part outline:**
  1. **Source:** USPTO guidance at 89 FR 25609 flags confidentiality, foreign filing, signatures/certifications, disclosure, accuracy, and supervision when AI tools are used in USPTO practice.
  2. **Audience decision:** name the intended task—brainstorming, search, drafting, translation, claim review, or administrative support—before selecting a tool.
  3. **Input decision:** ask what technical information, personal data, customer information, or third-party confidential material would enter the system and how vendor terms treat it.
  4. **Human-review decision:** assign the person responsible for checking accuracy, completeness, citations, technical meaning, and statements submitted to the USPTO.
  5. **Patent-strategy decision:** separately analyze natural-person inventorship under the USPTO’s November 2025 revised guidance, ownership/assignments, filing timing, and patent-versus-trade-secret choices.
  6. **Action:** run the AI Patent Strategy Scorecard without disclosing invention details, then decide whether a focused strategy review is warranted.
- **Cited source anchors:**
  - USPTO/Federal Register AI-tools guidance, 89 FR 25609 (Apr. 11, 2024): https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/04/11/2024-07629/guidance-on-use-of-artificial-intelligence-based-tools-in-practice-before-the-united-states-patent
  - USPTO AI-tools guidance video: https://www.uspto.gov/learning-and-resources/uspto-videos/guidance-use-ai-tools-practice-uspto
  - USPTO revised inventorship guidance (Nov. 26, 2025): https://www.uspto.gov/subscription-center/2025/revised-inventorship-guidance-ai-assisted-inventions

## 3) Deal-Ready Founder — Can Your AI Claims Survive Buyer Questions?

- **Primary keyword:** `AI due diligence checklist`
- **Search intent:** A founder, CEO, CFO, GC, investor, or acquirer wants a practical way to test AI product claims, data rights, vendor dependencies, and customer promises before a financing, partnership, or acquisition discussion.
- **Title options:**
  1. `AI Due Diligence: 6 Questions Before a Buyer Asks`
  2. `Can Your AI Product Claims Survive M&A Diligence?`
  3. `AI M&A Due Diligence for Founders: Claims, Data, Vendors, Customers`
- **Thumbnail hook:** `CAN YOU SUPPORT THE AI STORY?`
- **6-part outline:**
  1. **Source/consequence:** SEC enforcement materials illustrate the risk of false or misleading AI-use statements; the business consequence is credibility, valuation, and deal friction—not an automatic legal conclusion.
  2. **Claims decision:** identify the specific product, performance, automation, accuracy, and AI-use claims made to buyers, investors, customers, and the public; assign a business and technical owner for each.
  3. **Capability decision:** compare claims with the deployed product, human work in the loop, testing limits, known exceptions, and product roadmap.
  4. **Data/IP decision:** examine training, fine-tuning, retrieval, evaluation, and customer-input rights; connect this to ownership, confidentiality, privacy, and open-source/third-party components.
  5. **Vendor/customer decision:** review critical model/cloud dependencies, data-use promises, change/termination risk, customer contract commitments, and responsibility allocation. NIST’s GenAI Profile is voluntary guidance, not law or a safe harbor.
  6. **Action:** use the Founder M&A Readiness Checklist to prioritize non-financial questions before the other side controls the process.
- **Cited source anchors:**
  - SEC FY2025 enforcement results (Apr. 7, 2026), including an AI-company claims matter: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-34
  - SEC AI-washing enforcement release (Mar. 18, 2024): https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-36
  - FTC on AI privacy/confidentiality commitments: https://www.ftc.gov/policy/advocacy-research/tech-at-ftc/2024/01/ai-companies-uphold-your-privacy-confidentiality-commitments
  - NIST GenAI Profile, voluntary guidance; vendor due diligence and contract review: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf
