## 2. LinkedIn post draft

Colorado’s AI law delay should not become a governance delay.

SB25B-004 moved the effective date for the requirements created by SB24-205 to June 30, 2026. That gives executives breathing room — but not a reason to wait.

The companies in the strongest position next summer will not be the ones with the longest AI policy. They will be the ones that can produce a practical AI readiness file:

• AI systems in use, including embedded vendor tools
• use cases touching customers, employees, applicants, borrowers, tenants, patients, or other consequential decisions
• owners for approval, human review, escalation, and recordkeeping
• vendor terms for data, IP, confidentiality, audit, security, and indemnity risk
• a clear answer for boards, buyers, regulators, enterprise customers, and employees

That is a management problem before it is a legal memo problem.

For Colorado and Mountain West technology companies, the practical move is to use the runway to build the operating file now: inventory, ownership, vendor terms, human-review paths, records, and a prioritized cleanup list.

This is not about overreacting to unsettled law. It is about making AI adoption easier to defend, diligence, and scale.

## 3. Short email / newsletter draft

**Subject:** Colorado’s AI delay is a readiness runway  
**Preview text:** The companies best positioned for 2026 will have an AI operating file, not just an AI policy.

**Body:**

Colorado’s AI law timeline has shifted. SB25B-004 extends the effective date for the requirements created by SB24-205 to June 30, 2026.

For executives, that should create a practical question: what should we build before then?

The answer is usually not a 40-page policy first. A stronger starting point is an AI readiness file:

- an inventory of AI systems and embedded vendor tools;
- a map of use cases that may affect customers, employees, applicants, borrowers, tenants, patients, or other consequential decisions;
- clear owners for approval, human review, escalation, and recordkeeping;
- vendor-contract notes for data use, IP ownership, confidentiality, audit, security, and indemnity; and
- a prioritized cleanup list for legal, product, HR, procurement, and compliance.

That operating file helps leadership make better decisions now. It also makes the company easier to explain to boards, investors, buyers, enterprise customers, and regulators if questions arise later.

The law is still an area for careful, current review. But waiting until the deadline is not the only alternative to overreacting. A measured readiness process can reduce uncertainty without freezing AI adoption.
