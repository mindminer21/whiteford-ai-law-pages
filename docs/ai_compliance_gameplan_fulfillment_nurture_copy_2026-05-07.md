## 4. Readiness heat-map / priority-workstream modules

Use “priority workstream,” “readiness category,” “review signal,” or “operating gap” instead of definitive “legal risk” where possible. If “risk” appears in UI labels, define it as a triage signal rather than a legal conclusion.

### Heat-map intro copy

Your heat map highlights categories where your answers suggest the most organization value may exist. These are not legal findings. Use them as a starting point for organizing non-confidential records, assigning owners, and deciding whether company-specific legal review is needed.

### Category-specific modules

#### Use-Case Inventory

Priority label:

AI use-case and vendor inventory

Copy:

AI use often spreads across product, engineering, HR, sales, support, legal, finance, and operations before the company has one current map. The first governance step is usually visibility: which AI tools are used, who owns them, what data categories are touched, and whether outputs affect customers, employees, applicants, pricing, access, eligibility, or legal/financial decisions.

10-day action:

Create a non-confidential inventory with these columns: use-case category, team, owner, vendor/tool, embedded SaaS feature or standalone tool, data category, output audience, decision impact, current review status, and evidence location.

#### Vendor Risk

Priority label:

AI vendor and embedded-tool review

Copy:

AI vendor risk is often hidden inside normal SaaS adoption. A policy may not answer whether the vendor can use inputs for training, how logs are retained, what confidentiality obligations apply, who owns outputs, whether indemnity or audit rights exist, and how security obligations map to customer commitments.

10-day action:

Triage AI vendors into high, medium, and low review categories based on data sensitivity, customer/employee impact, output reliance, contract source, security posture, and whether the tool is embedded inside another platform.

#### Human Review

Priority label:

Human review, override, and escalation

Copy:

For consequential AI outputs, governance should not stop at “a human is in the loop.” The company needs to know who reviews, what they review, when they can override, how exceptions escalate, what record is retained, and how the process changes when outputs affect customers, employees, applicants, pricing, access, eligibility, legal, or financial decisions.

10-day action:

Draft a non-confidential human-review matrix: use-case category, reviewer role, decision type, override authority, escalation path, required record, and training owner.

#### Data Sensitivity

Priority label:

Sensitive data and AI data-flow map

Copy:

Prompting, uploads, fine-tuning, retrieval, logs, retention, output reuse, and vendor analytics can involve sensitive data even when the business team thinks of the tool as “just productivity software.” The score cannot determine legal obligations, but it can show whether the data-flow questions need a clearer owner.

10-day action:

Create a categorical data sensitivity matrix covering confidential business data, customer data, employee/applicant data, personal data, regulated data, source code, trade secrets, privileged materials, and data retained in prompts, logs, training, or retrieval systems.

#### Governance Owner

Priority label:

AI governance owner and approval authority

Copy:

A policy without an owner can become shelfware. Practical AI governance needs a person or committee with authority to coordinate intake, approvals, exceptions, vendor review, incident escalation, customer-facing claims, HR/employment review, training, evidence locations, and recurring reporting.

10-day action:

Name the governance owner, define intake thresholds, list cross-functional stakeholders, create an approval/escalation path, and identify the evidence repository.

#### Customer-Facing AI

Priority label:

Customer-facing AI features, claims, and escalation

Copy:

Customer-facing AI creates a different readiness question than internal productivity use. Product claims, output accuracy, disclaimers, support escalation, data use, security materials, sales promises, and customer-contract commitments should be aligned before launch or expansion.

10-day action:

Audit customer-facing AI surfaces by feature, user audience, output type, claim language, notice/disclaimer status, support escalation, vendor dependency, data category, and record owner.

#### Employment / HR AI

Priority label:

Employment, HR, applicant, and workforce AI

Copy:

AI used for recruiting, hiring, performance, scheduling, monitoring, compensation, or workforce analytics often deserves a separate workstream. Bias, notice, documentation, human review, records, vendor terms, and escalation questions can differ from product or back-office AI.

10-day action:

Inventory HR/workforce AI categorically and pause expansion of high-impact uses until review owners, notices, human-review rules, vendor terms, and recordkeeping steps are mapped.

#### Colorado / Mountain West

Priority label:

Colorado and Mountain West current-law readiness tracker

Copy:

Colorado and Mountain West AI, privacy, employment, consumer-protection, and recordkeeping requirements can change over time. The practical readiness move is not to declare the company compliant from a scorecard. It is to create a current-law review cadence tied to the company’s actual AI uses, vendors, notices, records, and decision-impact categories.

10-day action:

Create a Colorado/Mountain West readiness tracker with use-case category, jurisdictional exposure to review, owner, current-law source to confirm, notice/record category, review date, and next update date.

### Roadmap intro

### First 30 days: visibility and triage

Headline:

30 days: Make AI use visible enough to manage.

Copy:

The first 30 days should produce a current AI inventory, vendor list, data-category map, owner map, and first-pass heat map of customer-facing, employee/applicant-impacting, sensitive-data, and consequential-decision use cases. The goal is not a final legal conclusion. The goal is to know what exists, who owns it, what evidence is available, and which questions require company-specific review.

Potential outputs:

- AI use-case and vendor inventory.
- Data sensitivity matrix.
- Owner and evidence-location map.
- High-priority use-case list.
- Counsel-review question list without confidential public-form content.

### Days 31-60: controls and documentation

Headline:

60 days: Turn priority use cases into repeatable controls.

Copy:

The next 30 days should focus on the highest-priority workstreams surfaced by the heat map. For many technology companies, that means vendor terms, human review, sensitive-data handling, customer-facing AI claims, HR/employment AI, and records. The work should produce repeatable intake, approval, review, escalation, and documentation steps.

Potential outputs:

- Vendor review triage by risk category.
- Human-review and override matrix.
- Notice/disclaimer/customer-claim review queue.
- HR/employment AI review pathway if applicable.
- Evidence repository for policies, terms, approvals, and training.

### Days 61-90: operating cadence and leadership reporting

Headline:

90 days: Build the cadence so AI governance keeps up with AI adoption.

Copy:

By day 90, the company should be working toward a repeatable AI governance cadence: intake for new use cases, vendor and data review, escalation, training, evidence updates, current-law review, and leadership reporting. The goal is durable governance that can evolve as AI products, vendor terms, customer expectations, and legal obligations change.

Potential outputs:

- AI intake and approval workflow.
- Recurring governance meeting cadence.
- Board/executive-ready reporting summary.
- Training and update plan.
- Current-law review calendar.
- Reassessment date for the gameplan.

Roadmap CTA line:

If the roadmap feels too broad, start with the category most connected to the next business event: product launch, enterprise customer review, HR AI rollout, investor diligence, board update, or Colorado/Mountain West compliance planning.
