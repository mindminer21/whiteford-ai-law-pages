2. LinkedIn post

AI product launches are creating two evidence problems at once. On the IP side, founders need a clean record of what humans actually conceived, selected, refined, tested, and reduced to a working technical solution when AI tools helped the R&D process. On the governance side, executives need a clean record of where AI touches customers, employees, applicants, borrowers, tenants, or other consequential workflows — and which vendor, contract, data, notice, human-review, and recordkeeping controls apply. Those should not be separate fire drills. A practical AI launch file should answer three questions before the demo becomes a product promise: 1. What human technical contribution might be protectable? 2. What AI/data/vendor terms could affect ownership, confidentiality, or use rights? 3. Does the product make, guide, or assist decisions that require governance documentation or consumer-facing process? None of this determines patentability, inventorship, or compliance by itself. But it gives counsel and leadership the record needed to move deliberately instead of reconstructing the story during diligence, a customer security review, or a regulator question. For Colorado and Mountain West companies, AI readiness should become a launch discipline — not late-stage legal cleanup. Structured first pass: /ai-compliance-gameplan

3. Short email / newsletter

Subject: Before the AI demo becomes a legal record

Preview: A practical launch file can connect patent, vendor, and Colorado AI governance issues before they become diligence problems.

Many AI legal problems are not caused by the model itself. They are caused by missing records.

When an AI-enabled product moves from prototype to customer-facing workflow, leadership should be able to answer three questions: who made the human technical contribution, which vendor or data terms may affect rights and confidentiality, and whether the system touches consequential decisions that require governance, notice, human review, or records.

This is not a request for confidential facts in a public form, and it is not a substitute for legal advice. It is a structured way to identify where counsel should focus first.
