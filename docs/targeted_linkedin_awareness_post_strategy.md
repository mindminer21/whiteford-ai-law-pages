# Targeted LinkedIn Awareness Post Strategy

Generated: 2026-05-06
Status: draft/internal strategy. No LinkedIn/social posting, tagging, commenting, DM, or automation is authorized by this document.

## Operating Decision

Every prospect-facing strategy, Agent Targeting packet, and authority-content plan should include at least one draft LinkedIn awareness post that uses a public company event or sourced fact to teach an issue connected to one Whiteford/Schell IP lead magnet.

The post should be written for Jeff's LinkedIn profile and may tag the company and, when appropriate and approved, one key public contact. The goal is not direct solicitation. The goal is public educational awareness that helps the tagged company and adjacent buyers recognize a business/legal issue and find the relevant lead magnet.

## Safety Rule

Drafting is approved. Posting is not.

Agents may research public facts, draft the post, create a creative brief, attach a lead-magnet CTA, and queue the draft for Jeff/Whiteford review. Agents must not:

- publish or schedule the post;
- tag a company or person live;
- comment on company/contact posts;
- send LinkedIn messages;
- connect with prospects;
- use LinkedIn automation;
- imply that the tagged company has a legal problem;
- imply legal advice, patentability, compliance, deal success, or an attorney-client relationship.

## Required Pattern

Each draft should include:

1. **Public-source hook:** one verifiable company event, product launch, funding, hiring, patent/publication, partnership, customer announcement, regulatory trigger, or market trend.
2. **Company tag:** draft syntax for tagging the company. Use placeholder format if the exact LinkedIn handle is not verified: `@[Company LinkedIn Page - verify handle]`.
3. **Key contact tag, optional:** only if the person is a public-facing executive/founder and Jeff approves individual tagging. Use placeholder format: `@[Name - verify LinkedIn profile/tag]`.
4. **Business insight:** explain why the event matters beyond the company.
5. **Legal/business bridge:** connect to one of the four lead-magnet themes:
   - AI Patent Strategy Scorecard.
   - AI Compliance Gameplan Builder.
   - Tech Company Legal Health Checklist.
   - Founder M&A Readiness Checklist.
6. **Audience segmentation:** 2-4 short “For ___” lines, e.g., founders, GCs, investors, product leaders, CFOs.
7. **Soft CTA:** link to the relevant lead magnet as an educational resource. Use no pressure, no guarantee, and no “you need a lawyer” framing.
8. **Evidence block for review:** source URLs and what each source supports. This evidence block stays internal and is not posted.

## Example: AI Patent Strategy

```text
@[Company - verify handle] is a useful signal for a bigger AI patent issue:

AI products are becoming faster to demonstrate than they are to legally organize.

That sounds like a patent filing problem.

It isn't only that.

The harder question is whether the company can later explain:

1. what humans contributed,
2. what the AI system contributed,
3. what should be patented,
4. what should stay trade secret, and
5. what was publicly disclosed before the strategy was set.

For #founders: invention records are becoming part of the product operating system.
For #investors: AI diligence will increasingly ask whether the moat is documented, not just whether the demo is impressive.
For #productleaders: roadmap, disclosure, contractor terms, and patent strategy need to move together.

Educational starting point: AI Patent Strategy Scorecard
https://whiteford-ai-law-pages.vercel.app/ai-patent-strategy-scorecard
```

## Example: AI Compliance

```text
@[Company - verify handle] is a useful example of how AI is moving from feature to operating layer.

That changes the legal question.

The question is not simply, "Do we have an AI policy?"

It is whether the company can identify:

1. where AI is used,
2. what data enters the workflow,
3. who owns the decision,
4. whether a human reviews the output,
5. which vendor terms matter, and
6. what records would explain the process later.

For #founders: AI governance should be lightweight enough to use.
For #operators: vendor intake and use-case inventory matter before the memo.
For #boards: the useful artifact is a roadmap, not a panic document.

Educational starting point: AI Compliance Gameplan Builder
https://whiteford-ai-law-pages.vercel.app/ai-compliance-gameplan
```

## Example: Tech Outside GC

```text
@[Company - verify handle] is a good reminder that scaling a technology company creates legal work in layers.

The patent strategy matters.

But so do the contract stack, employee/contractor documents, AI/data intake, customer commitments, privacy/security review, and financing readiness.

That sounds like a long legal list.

It should be treated as an operating system.

For #founders: the issue is sequencing, not doing everything at once.
For #COOs: legal work should map to the next business milestone.
For #investors: clean operating infrastructure reduces diligence drag.

Educational starting point: Tech Company Legal Health Checklist
```

## Example: Founder M&A Readiness

```text
@[Company - verify handle] is a useful signal for founders thinking about strategic deals.

M&A readiness is not just a banker conversation.

The buyer's questions often start with non-financial issues:

1. who owns the IP,
2. whether contractors assigned what they built,
3. whether customer contracts transfer cleanly,
4. whether AI/data risks are documented,
5. whether employee records and approvals are clean, and
6. whether the story survives diligence.

For #founders: cleanup is easier before the buyer asks.
For #CFOs: diligence friction can become business friction.
For #investors: legal readiness changes the quality of the process.

Educational starting point: Founder M&A Readiness Checklist
```

## Approval Gate

Before any LinkedIn post goes live, Jeff or an authorized human reviewer must approve:

1. exact post copy;
2. exact company tag;
3. exact person tag, if any;
4. source grounding;
5. lead-magnet URL;
6. legal/ethics/brand posture;
7. timing and channel;
8. whether Whiteford review is needed.
